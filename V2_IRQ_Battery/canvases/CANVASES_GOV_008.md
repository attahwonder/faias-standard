# CANVAS: IRQ-GOV-008

## 1. IRQ ID & Title
**IRQ-GOV-008: Operational Monitoring Signals Not Reaching Accountable Governance Bodies**

Register question: "Do monitoring results, incidents and drift signals flow to accountable governance bodies with defined triggers for revalidation, restriction or withdrawal?"

## 2. Business Activities Affected
- **Every AI system with an operational monitoring capability:** monitoring is only a safety control if its outputs reach a body with authority to act; a dashboard nobody with decision rights reviews is instrumentation, not governance.
- **Systems approaching or exceeding tested drift/quality thresholds:** IRQ-RES-005/-006 may correctly detect and even alert on degradation, but if no defined escalation path routes that alert to a governance body with revalidation/restriction/withdrawal authority, detection achieves nothing.
- **Multi-deployment estates:** where the same model or template is deployed across several business lines, a drift signal in one deployment should prompt governance to ask whether the same weakness exists elsewhere — this cross-deployment escalation is a governance function, not a monitoring one.

## 3. AI Behaviour(s)
- N/A at the model-behaviour level — this IRQ concerns whether the organisation's governance bodies actually consume operational signals the technical monitoring layer already produces, not the technical detection capability itself (homed in RES G.3).

## 4. Failure Mode
> Monitoring, incident and drift signals generated by the operational layer (IRQ-RES-005/-006) exist but do not reach an accountable governance body with defined triggers and authority to revalidate, restrict or withdraw the system — so detection capability exists on paper while the organisational escalation path that would convert a signal into action is absent, informal, or too slow to be effective.

## 5. Potential Consequences
- **Customer:** A system continues operating under conditions its own monitoring has flagged as degraded (rising hallucination rate, drift beyond threshold, repeated incidents) because no governance body with authority to restrict or withdraw it ever saw the signal.
- **Institution:** The firm cannot credibly claim ongoing assurance for a deployed system if its monitoring output is not reviewed at governance level; in a post-incident investigation, "the dashboard showed it" is not a defence if no accountable body consumed the dashboard.
- **Regulator/market:** PRA SS1/23's ongoing-monitoring principle and the FCA/PRA operational resilience regime both expect operational signals to inform governance decisions, not merely exist as telemetry; a gap here is a direct governance-process finding, distinct from (and often masking) an otherwise-functioning technical monitoring capability.

## 6. Failure Chain
```
Monitoring layer correctly detects drift, quality decay or an incident
  ↓ (IRQ-RES-005/-006: detection and alerting capability functions as designed)
Alert is generated but routed only to a technical/operations distribution list,
with no defined escalation trigger to a governance body holding revalidation,
restriction or withdrawal authority
  ↓ (IRQ-GOV-008 — THIS IRQ: signal does not reach accountable governance)
No governance review occurs; no revalidation, restriction or withdrawal decision
is made despite the underlying signal meeting or exceeding its own defined threshold
  ↓ (IRQ-GOV-001: without a clear owner, there is no obvious recipient for escalation
  even if a route existed)
System continues operating in a degraded state; degradation compounds silently
  ↓
Customer harm accumulates across the undetected-at-governance-level period →
post-incident review finds the monitoring signal existed months before any
governance action was taken → finding of ineffective ongoing oversight

Control Breakpoints:
  • IRQ-RES-005/-006: the upstream detection capability this IRQ depends upon
  • IRQ-GOV-008: defined escalation triggers with governance-level receipt and authority
  • IRQ-GOV-001: named ownership gives the escalation path an accountable destination
  • IRQ-GOV-006: governance decisions on escalated signals are tested against risk appetite
```

## 7. Why a Domain Expert May Miss It
- SMEs experienced with traditional model monitoring assume that "monitoring exists" is equivalent to "monitoring informs governance"; the organisational plumbing between a technical alert and a committee agenda item is easy to overlook because it is process, not technology.
- The RES/GOV boundary is genuinely subtle: a reviewer assessing monitoring capability (does the dashboard exist, are thresholds sensible) can reasonably conclude the control is present, without separately asking whether anyone with authority to act actually receives and reviews it.
- Escalation triggers are often defined in a policy document but not actually wired into governance committee terms of reference or meeting cadence — a documentation/practice gap invisible without tracing a specific incident or drift event through to its governance disposition.
- GenAI/agentic monitoring produces novel signal types (hallucination rate, prompt drift, escalation-workflow SLA breaches) that legacy governance committee agendas were not designed to receive; a traditional model-governance committee may simply not have an item for "GenAI operational signals" on its standing agenda.

## 8. Assurance Objective
**AO-GOV-008:** Monitoring results, incidents and drift signals for deployed AI systems shall flow to a named, accountable governance body through a defined escalation trigger, with documented authority and evidenced practice of acting (revalidation, restriction or withdrawal) on signals that meet threshold.

## 9. Expected Controls (Traced)
- **Defined escalation triggers routing monitoring/incident/drift signals to a named governance body with revalidation, restriction and withdrawal authority** — [PRA, SS1/23, Principle 4 (Independent model validation), which carries ongoing monitoring and periodic review; restriction and withdrawal outcomes then engage Principle 5 (Model risk mitigants)] — the primary anchor: SS1/23 requires monitoring outcomes to inform governance decisions on model status, extended here to AI/ML.
- **Governance committee terms of reference explicitly including AI operational signals as a standing agenda item** — [ISO/IEC 42001:2023, clause 9.3 (management review)] — management review must consider performance and monitoring information; without an explicit standing item, AI-specific signals can be structurally absent from review scope.
- **Escalation trigger testing: at least one simulated or real signal traced end-to-end from detection to governance disposition** — [FCA/PRA operational resilience regime, PRA SS1/21; FCA PS21/3] — operational resilience expectations require tested, not merely designed, escalation paths for material service-affecting events.
- **Cross-deployment escalation for template/shared-model drift signals** — [Emerging Practice] — no specific published standard names cross-deployment escalation for shared AI components explicitly; this control is included as good practice given the concentration risk shared components create, and marked accordingly.
- **Governance decision log evidencing revalidation/restriction/withdrawal actions actually taken in response to escalated signals** — [PRA, SS1/23, Principle 5] — a functioning escalation control produces a record of governance decisions, not only a record of signals generated.

## 10. Expected Evidence (Tagged L1–L5)
- Policy statement that "monitoring feeds governance" — **L1** (assertion; does not evidence an actual route or trigger).
- Escalation-trigger design document specifying signal types, thresholds and destination governance body — **L2**.
- Governance committee terms of reference showing AI operational signals as a standing or recurring agenda item — **L2**.
- Traced test: a specific historical or simulated drift/incident signal followed end-to-end from generation to governance committee minute recording a decision — **L3**.
- Internal audit review of the escalation control's design and operating effectiveness — **L4**.
- Governance decision log (last 12 months): record of revalidation/restriction/withdrawal decisions made in response to escalated signals, with dates and disposition — **L5**.

## 11. Independent Test
> Select a sample of historical monitoring alerts, drift-threshold breaches and incidents recorded by the operational monitoring layer (RES G.3) across a sample of deployed AI systems. For each, trace forward: (1) whether an escalation trigger fired, (2) which governance body, if any, received the signal, (3) the time elapsed between signal generation and governance-level review, and (4) what decision, if any, governance made. Separately, review governance committee terms of reference and minutes for the presence of AI operational signals as a standing item, and interview committee members on whether they recall reviewing AI-specific monitoring output. Methodology follows standard escalation-path reconciliation and control-testing practice (as applied to operational resilience incident-escalation testing), extended to the AI-specific signal population with no AI-specific benchmark for acceptable escalation latency. Conducted against governance records, committee minutes and interviews only; no production system access required. — **★3 Confidence — as at FAIAS v1.1.0 (25 August 2026) · P3 (§6A.4)** (credible, established escalation-path reconciliation methodology adapted from operational resilience testing practice; no AI-specific standard defines acceptable escalation latency or governance review adequacy for AI signals specifically, so professional judgement determines whether the traced path constitutes effective escalation).

## 12. Pass Indicators
- 100% of sampled monitoring alerts/drift breaches/incidents are traceable to a governance-body review within the firm's own defined SLA.
- Governance committee terms of reference and minutes confirm AI operational signals are a standing, actively-reviewed agenda item.
- Governance decision log shows at least one instance of revalidation, restriction or withdrawal action taken in direct response to an escalated signal in the review period.
- Interviewed governance body members can describe recent AI monitoring signals they reviewed and the decisions taken.
- Cross-deployment escalation evidenced for at least one shared-component drift signal (where applicable).

## 13. Fail Indicators
- Sampled alerts/breaches/incidents show no trace of governance-level review; the escalation path exists in policy but not in practice.
- Governance committee minutes contain no reference to AI operational signals despite monitoring having generated qualifying alerts in the period.
- Governance decision log shows only "noted" entries with no evidence of revalidation, restriction or withdrawal decisions ever taken.
- Escalation latency, where traceable, materially exceeds the firm's own defined SLA with no compensating control.
- Interviewed governance body members are unaware that AI-specific monitoring signals exist or should be reaching them.

## 14. Reporting Language
**Pass:** "Monitoring, incident and drift signals for the sampled AI systems are demonstrated to reach [named governance body] through a defined and tested escalation route; the body's terms of reference and minutes confirm active review, and its decision log evidences [n] revalidation/restriction actions taken in the review period. Conclusions rest on ★3 methodology — established escalation-path reconciliation practice with no AI-specific benchmark — and should be read as professional assessment."
**Fail:** "Testing found that monitoring alerts and drift-threshold breaches for [n] of [sample size] systems generated no traceable governance-level review; the escalation route described in policy is not operating in practice. This is a material gap: operational monitoring capability exists but does not translate into governance action, meaning degraded systems can continue operating unchecked. Remediation should establish and test an actual escalation route before reliance is placed on this control."
**Partial:** "Escalation to governance is demonstrated for incident-level signals but not for gradual drift-threshold breaches, which are logged but not routed with the same urgency. The assurance objective is not yet met for drift-type signals; remediation should extend the tested escalation route to all signal types within [n] days."

## 15. Residual Judgement
```
Assuming controls are well-implemented and monitored:
  Severity: 2 (an escalation gap compounds whatever underlying weakness the signal
              was meant to surface; standalone severity is moderate)
  Likelihood: 2 (plausible where governance committees have not yet adapted standing
              agendas to AI-specific signal types; less likely with a tested route)
  Detectability: 3 (only found by tracing specific signals through to governance
              disposition; a policy document describing the route looks adequate
              without this tracing)
  Recoverability: 1 (once found, wiring an actual escalation route and testing it is
              a process fix, achievable quickly)
  Novelty: 3 (a governance SME may assume the existing model-governance committee
              structure automatically extends to AI-specific signal types without
              checking that its standing agenda was actually updated)

Interpretation: acceptable residual risk once a tested, evidenced escalation route
exists; the principal concern is that this gap silently defeats the value of an
otherwise well-functioning monitoring capability (IRQ-RES-005/-006), so it should
be assessed together with, not instead of, those IRQs.

If controls are absent or degraded:
  Severity: 3 (systems continue operating in a demonstrably degraded state because
              no governance action is ever triggered)
  Likelihood: 3 (foreseeable where committee structures have not been updated for
              AI-specific signal types, which is common in the current transition
              period)
  Detectability: 4 (a monitoring dashboard that exists but is never escalated is
              indistinguishable from a functioning control until a specific incident
              is traced end-to-end)
  Recoverability: 2 (remediable by establishing and testing the route, but the
              undetected-at-governance-level period cannot be retrospectively cured)
  Novelty: 3
Residual risk: High if the escalation route is untested; this IRQ determines whether
IRQ-RES-005/-006's detection capability produces any governance consequence at all.
```

## 17. Regulatory Basis
> [PRA, SS1/23, Principle 4 (Independent model validation) — ongoing monitoring and periodic review; restriction/withdrawal engages Principle 5 (Model risk mitigants)] requires monitoring outcomes to inform governance decisions on model status, use restriction or withdrawal; extended to AI/ML per PRA's April 2024 mapping letter. [FCA/PRA operational resilience regime, PRA SS1/21; FCA PS21/3] requires tested escalation paths for events materially affecting important business services, which applies where AI system degradation could do so. [ISO/IEC 42001:2023, clause 9.3] requires management review of AI system performance and monitoring information as a standing governance activity.
