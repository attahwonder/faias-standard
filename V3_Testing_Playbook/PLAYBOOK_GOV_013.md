# TP-GOV-013 — Synthetic-Signature Cohort Enumeration Demonstration

## 1. Procedure Identity
**Procedure ID:** TP-GOV-013
**IRQ:** IRQ-GOV-013 — Complaint-Led Remediation Blind to the Full Affected Cohort
**Assurance objective:** AO-GOV-013 — Given a defined failure signature (model/configuration version, prompt or logic defect, affected date range), the firm shall be able to identify the full affected cohort of decisions and shall operate a proactive remediation process that reaches that cohort without depending on an inbound complaint.
**★ Confidence (carried from canvas, unchanged):** ★2 — the underlying harm, complaint-confined redress leaving an affected cohort unremediated, is well evidenced by the Robodebt Royal Commission finding (Report, Vol. 1, 7 July 2023, p. v), but the test method itself is bespoke, with **no published protocol, benchmark or established sampling frame for "cohort-identification completeness"**; the assessment rests heavily on reviewer professional judgement.
**Method classification:** Emerging Practice / bespoke — a capability demonstration, not an audit of records, conducted entirely in test/staging. The canvas states plainly that no published protocol exists for this test, and the harm anchor is a public-inquiry precedent rather than a research methodology. Two of its five expected controls — the queryable decision store indexed by defect signature, and the rehearsal of the capability against a synthetic or historical signature — are marked Emerging Practice, the latter included on the reasoning that an untested capability offers no more assurance than an untested disaster-recovery plan.

## 2. Preconditions
**Environment:** Governance records plus a test or staging environment holding a representative decision store. **The demonstration runs entirely in test/staging with no production data and no live customer contact at any point** — nothing this procedure does reaches, contacts or affects any real customer, whether or not they are in a real affected cohort.
**Access required:** The decision store's query interface as it would actually be used in a remediation exercise, not a bespoke query written for the reviewer by an engineer with database access; the decision store's schema or field documentation, specifically which fields are queryable — model version, configuration, prompt, defect signature, date range; configuration or code access sufficient to confirm the query capability returns population-level results, not only lookup by decision ID; the governance record defining the proactive remediation trigger and its ownership; any rehearsal or historical exercise log; the individual complaint-handling process design, so that the two can be compared rather than conflated.
**Artefacts required:** The reviewer-constructed synthetic defect signature; design documentation of the decision store's query capability, specifying which fields can be queried and whether the query returns a complete population or a single record; configuration or code evidence that population-level query is implemented; the governance record naming ownership and the trigger for proactive remediation independent of inbound complaint; any rehearsal or exercise log; evidence of any actual historical instance where a defect was identified and the full cohort proactively remediated.
**Personnel required:** Reviewer (lead); the owner of the decision store or remediation platform, to execute the demonstration; the complaint-handling process owner; the named owner of cohort-level remediation, **if one exists** — establishing whether that role exists at all, distinct from individual complaint-handling ownership, is one of the findings.
**Data handling:** The synthetic failure signature the reviewer supplies must not correspond to a real known defect, to avoid the exercise being read as an assertion about a live population. Where the staging decision store contains de-identified production records, handle under engagement confidentiality; the reviewer records cohort **counts and query performance**, not the identities of cohort members. No real customer record is remediated, contacted or altered.

## 3. Test Design
**A supplied-signature capability demonstration, timed and scored for completeness.** The reviewer supplies a synthetic defect signature — a model or configuration version, a defined date range, and a stated logic or prompt defect — **not previously used by the firm for this purpose** — and asks the firm to demonstrate enumeration of the *full* cohort of decisions matching it. A signature the firm has rehearsed against measures rehearsal, not capability.

The distinction the whole procedure turns on: **retrieval of one named record is not cohort enumeration.** The firm may be able to reconstruct any decision on request — that is TP-GOV-010's capability, and it is a necessary precondition here — and still have no way to work backwards from a failure signature to a population. The two sound adjacent and require entirely different query architecture: per-decision key lookup against signature-based cohort enumeration.

**Signature-field coverage.** The reviewer supplies a signature that spans **all** the fields the assurance objective names — model/configuration version, prompt or logic defect, and date range — because a store queryable by version alone but not by prompt or logic-defect signature is the canvas's own partial condition. Where the firm can enumerate on some fields and not others, that is recorded field by field, not as a single pass or fail.

**Timing and manual-step recording.** The firm states the time taken and **any manual steps required, and who performed them**. Cohort identification that is possible only through extensive manual case-by-case investigation, with no defined query capability or measurable timeframe, is a canvas fail indicator even where a cohort is eventually produced.

**Documented-versus-demonstrated comparison.** The documented query capability is recorded **before** the exercise and compared against what is demonstrated — the domain's second discipline, applied so the reviewer is not anchored to the firm's own account of its capability.

**Trigger limb, assessed separately from the query limb.** A proactive remediation trigger, structurally independent of an inbound complaint, is a distinct control from the ability to enumerate. Establish whether one exists, whether ownership is distinct from individual complaint-handling ownership, and — applying the domain's "existence is not operation" discipline — whether it has **activated at least once historically** without waiting for a complaint from any cohort member.

**Rehearsal limb.** Establish whether the capability has been rehearsed by the firm itself against a synthetic or historical signature, with completeness and time-to-identify recorded, or only designed. The canvas records "no rehearsal or historical exercise evidence exists; the capability, if claimed, has never actually been tested" as a distinct fail indicator.

**Completeness caveat, stated in the report.** The reviewer can confirm that the returned cohort matches the supplied signature. Confirming that it is **complete** depends on the staging store's own fidelity, and no established sampling frame for cohort-identification completeness exists. State the basis on which completeness was assessed, and its limits.

## 4. Steps
1. Confirm the staging environment and its decision store are representative, and record the basis. **Record:** the store's coverage relative to production and any known gap, which bounds every completeness statement that follows.
2. Construct the synthetic failure signature spanning model/configuration version, a stated prompt or logic defect, and a date range. Confirm it does not correspond to a known real defect and has not previously been used by the firm for this purpose. **Record:** the signature and the confirmation, supplied to the firm only at the point of the exercise.
3. Before the exercise, obtain the decision store's design documentation and record which fields it states are queryable, and whether the query is complete-population or single-record. **Record:** the documented capability, against which the demonstrated capability will be compared.
4. Supply the signature and observe the enumeration attempt in test/staging. **Record:** whether the full matching cohort was enumerated or only individual records retrieved; the cohort count returned; the elapsed time; every manual step required, and who performed it — a query run by the one engineer who can write it is a different capability from one a remediation team can run.
5. Test field-by-field enumeration: repeat with a signature restricted to version only, then to prompt or logic defect only, then to date range only. **Record:** which fields support enumeration and which do not.
6. Confirm by configuration or code review that population-level query is actually implemented, not only lookup by decision ID, and compare the demonstrated capability against the documented capability recorded at step 3. **Record:** the implemented capability and any divergence in either direction.
7. Establish whether the firm can enumerate at all beyond retrieval of the single named record. **Record:** the distinction explicitly — the canvas's headline fail indicator is exactly the ability to reconstruct one decision and nothing more.
8. Obtain the governance record for the proactive remediation trigger. **Record:** whether it exists, what activates it, whether it is structurally independent of inbound complaint, and who owns it.
9. Establish whether cohort-level remediation ownership is distinct from individual complaint-handling ownership, or defaults to it. **Record:** the named owner, or the default.
10. Search for evidence that the proactive trigger has activated at least once historically without waiting for a complaint. **Record:** the instance and its outcome, or the absence of any.
11. Obtain any rehearsal or exercise log. **Record:** the signature used, the completeness recorded, the time-to-identify, and the date — or that the capability has never been rehearsed.
12. Establish the relationship the firm asserts between this capability and its complaint-handling framework. **Record:** whether cohort-level identification is treated as additional to the complaint-initiated route, or as satisfied by it.

## 6. Interpretation (per CANVASES_GOV.md fields 12–14)
**Pass** where the firm enumerates the full synthetic cohort from the supplied failure signature within a reasonable, stated timeframe and without manual case-by-case investigation; query capability spans model/configuration/prompt version, logic defect and date range rather than a subset; a proactive remediation trigger exists and is evidenced to have activated at least once historically without waiting for a complaint; named ownership of cohort-level remediation is distinct from, and does not default to, individual complaint-handling ownership; and the exercise log shows the capability has been rehearsed, not only designed.

**Fail** where: the firm can reconstruct only the individual decision supplied and has no mechanism to enumerate other decisions sharing the same signature; **or** cohort identification, where possible at all, requires extensive manual investigation with no defined query capability or measurable timeframe; **or** no proactive remediation trigger exists and remediation is confirmed to occur only in response to an inbound complaint; **or** the decision store cannot be queried by model/configuration/prompt version or defect signature, only by customer or decision identifier; **or** no rehearsal or historical exercise evidence exists and the capability, if claimed, has never actually been tested.

**Partial** where — the canvas's own shape — the firm can enumerate a cohort by model or configuration version but not by prompt or logic-defect signature specifically, and the proactive trigger has been designed but never exercised. The assurance objective is not yet met; remediation should extend query capability to all signature fields and rehearse the proactive trigger. Report the field-by-field result from step 5, which is what makes this finding actionable rather than directional.

## 7. Evidence Outputs
- Statement that any decision can be reconstructed on request — **L1** (evidences per-decision capability only; does not evidence cohort-level query capability)
- Design documentation of the decision store's query capability, specifying which fields can be queried and whether the query is complete-population or single-record — **L2**
- Governance record showing named ownership and a defined trigger for proactive remediation, independent of inbound complaint — **L2/L3**
- Reviewer's synthetic failure signature and the confirmation it does not correspond to a known real defect and has not previously been used — **L3**
- Configuration or code review confirming population-level query is implemented, not just lookup by decision ID — **L3**
- Exercise record: cohort count, elapsed time, every manual step required and who performed it, with the field-by-field enumeration result and the divergence against the documented capability — **L3**
- Firm's own rehearsal or exercise log: a synthetic or historical signature supplied and the full matching cohort enumerated, with completeness and time-to-identify recorded — **L3**
- Reviewer's stated basis for assessing completeness, with its limits — **L3**
- Evidence from an actual historical instance where a defect was identified and the firm proactively remediated the full cohort without waiting for complaints from every member — **L5** (demonstrates the mechanism functions, not merely exists)

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Limitations
- **Accepting reconstruction as enumeration.** "We can reconstruct any decision on request" answers TP-GOV-010's question. It is a necessary precondition here and is not an answer to this one, and the two are easily conflated because they sound adjacent while requiring entirely different query architecture.
- **Accepting a hand-written query.** If the enumeration requires the one engineer who can write it, the firm does not have a remediation capability; it has a person. Record who ran the query and whether a remediation team could.
- **Supplying a signature the firm has seen.** A previously used signature may have a cached or pre-built query behind it; the demonstration then measures preparation, not capability. Step 2's novelty requirement is not a formality.
- **Testing on version only.** A store queryable by model version but not by prompt or logic-defect signature will pass a lazy exercise and fail the real one, which is why step 5 runs field by field.
- **Treating a designed trigger as a control.** The domain's third discipline applies with full force: a trigger that has never activated has never been shown to do anything. The historical-activation evidence is the substance of the trigger limb.
- **The aggregate-monitoring assumption.** A single bad prompt version or threshold mis-set can be far more surgical in its effect than a miscalibrated score, and much less visible in aggregate metrics. "Monitoring would have caught a cohort-wide issue" does not transfer.
- **Overstating completeness.** The reviewer can confirm the cohort matches the signature. Whether it is the complete population depends on the staging store's fidelity, and no benchmark for completeness exists. Say what was confirmed and what was not.
- **Accepting complaint-handling compliance as an answer.** FCA DISP 1.1.3R (as at 31/07/2026) binds the firm once a complaint arrives from an eligible complainant. It does not require the firm to look for uncomplained members of an affected cohort, and that gap is precisely what this procedure tests.
- **Regulatory-basis limitation carried forward.** The canvas records that **no UK or EU regulatory instrument imposing a proactive, signature-based cohort-identification duty specific to AI-influenced decisions was identified**, and that the control area rests on an Australian public-inquiry precedent rather than a binding UK/EU rule. Report the finding against the assurance objective, not as a regulatory breach.
- **★2 caveat.** A bespoke capability demonstration with no published protocol, no benchmark and no established sampling frame for completeness. Report as professional assessment, not benchmarked assurance.

## 9. Time / Effort Estimate
**4 reviewer-days**: 0.5 day staging representativeness confirmation and design-documentation review; 0.5 day synthetic signature construction; 1 day exercise observation including the field-by-field repetition and manual-step recording (coordination-heavy — it requires the decision-store owner's time and a working staging store); 0.5 day configuration or code review and documented-versus-demonstrated comparison; 0.75 day trigger, ownership and historical-activation limbs; 0.25 day rehearsal-log review; 0.5 day completeness-basis reasoning, analysis and working papers.
