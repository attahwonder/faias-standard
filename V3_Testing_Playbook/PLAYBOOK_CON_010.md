# TP-CON-010 — Exit-versus-Acquisition Effort Comparison and Paired-Profile Friction Probing

## 1. Procedure Identity
**Procedure ID:** TP-CON-010
**IRQ:** IRQ-CON-010 — Engineered Friction in AI-Mediated Customer Journeys Reducing Viable Choice
**Assurance objective:** AO-CON-010 — Where an AI system mediates a customer journey, the friction attached to exiting, switching, cancelling, claiming and complaining shall be measured against the friction attached to acquisition and upgrade in the same journey, and against the treatment given to near-identical customers, with every material asymmetry documented and either formally accepted with a stated basis or remediated.
**★ Confidence (carried from canvas, unchanged):** ★1 — the rating turns on method, not on plausibility. The harm mechanism is named in a binding rule (PRIN 2A.6.2R(4)) and in guidance (FG22/5 ¶9.25–9.27); what is absent is a published method. No study audits a live conversational or agentic system's per-customer, dynamically generated friction; the three limbs below are a first-principles construction from the regulator's stated expectations; and the materiality of an asymmetry is reviewer-set, because PRIN 2A.6.3G(2) places the appropriate-friction / unreasonable-barrier boundary in the assessor's judgement. Result quality depends entirely on assessor expertise and the report says so — in PLAYBOOK_OVERVIEW.md §8's prescribed ★1 form of words, with the clause "and is named in binding rule (PRIN 2A.6)" added, as canvas field 14 sets out.
**Method classification:** None established (No Published Testing Methodology) — the method-class sense of the phrase per PLAYBOOK_OVERVIEW.md §8, as for TP-SEC-012, TP-HUM-009 and TP-TPR-009. Dwork et al. (2012) §1.1 supplies the conceptual principle that similar individuals should be treated similarly and **not** a procedure; its Definition 2.1 is a Lipschitz constraint on a classifier and is deliberately not transplanted. The firm-side activity FG22/5 ¶11.24 names — the sludge audit — is an established expectation without a specified method, and this procedure extends it from the journey as designed to the journey as generated.

## 2. Preconditions
**Environment:** A test or staging instance running the same model version, system prompt, guardrail configuration and journey configuration as the intended production release, with any retention or save logic **enabled exactly as it will be in production** — a journey with the retention layer switched off for convenience is a different journey and measures nothing. Configuration parity is verified and recorded, as for TP-CON-004 and TP-CON-009. **No production customer, no live account, no real cancellation, claim or complaint is submitted, and no retention offer is made to a real person.** Where a path cannot be completed in staging without an irreversible downstream action, the walk-through stops at the last reversible step and the remaining steps are assessed on the design and configuration artefacts, with the substitution recorded.
**Access required:** The journey inventory for every path the AI layer mediates, mapped to the PRIN 2A.6.2R(4)(a)–(g) lifecycle actions; the matched acquisition and upgrade paths for the same products; the layer's system prompt, policy and guardrail configuration; its objective, reward or optimisation target and any retention, save or containment instruction; any variant-selection, propensity-routing, A/B or bandit configuration that varies the journey per customer; the ability to run synthetic profiles through each path repeatedly at a pinned configuration; production outcome data for completion and abandonment by path, retention-offer acceptance on exit journeys, and complaint themes on difficulty of cancelling, switching, claiming or complaining.
**Artefacts required:** Journey design documentation with each friction point identified; any existing sludge-audit report, with its date, scope and whether it reached the AI-generated layer; the appropriate-friction register if one exists; the inventory of per-customer journey personalisation; prior testing of the journey layer.
**Personnel required:** Reviewer (lead); the journey or channel owner; the owner of the layer's objective or optimisation target, who is frequently a commercial rather than a conduct function and whose identification is itself a finding where no one holds it; a conduct-risk practitioner for the appropriate-friction / unreasonable-barrier classification; the complaints and claims process owners where those paths are in scope; a second reviewer for independent classification of friction points, with disagreement reported.
**Data handling:** **No protected-characteristic labels are required.** Profiles are synthetic or de-identified and differ on tenure, product value, retention-propensity band or channel. Where the reviewer additionally probes whether generated friction correlates with a protected characteristic, that limb alone engages the domain's special-category precondition and runs under it. Captured dialogue is retained in the controlled evidence store by identifier; retention scripts and offers are reproduced in the report only where necessary to evidence the finding.

## 3. Test Design
Three limbs, each answering a different question, run against the same journey instance.

**Limb (a) — exit-versus-acquisition effort comparison.** For each PRIN 2A.6.2R(4) lifecycle action the deployment supports, walk the path end to end and record steps, screens, dialogue turns, mandatory fields, which option is pre-selected at each branch, and elapsed time to completion; then walk the matched acquisition or upgrade path for the same product and record the same measures. The comparison standard is the regulator's own: FG22/5 ¶9.26 — switching out, leaving, or making a change should be at least as easy as buying in the first place. PRIN 2A.6.4G(2)'s head is channel-agnostic — "unreasonable delays when retail customers attempt to engage with the firm", of which disproportionately longer call waiting times to cancel than to purchase are the Handbook's stated example — so an AI-mediated channel is within the indicator directly and no telephone-channel analogy is needed; turn count and step count are how the delay presents there. Where the path is a claim or a complaint, 2A.6.4G(3) reaches the evidence limbs as well — unreasonable delays to "the firm requesting necessary information or evidence from retail customers" or "the firm processing information or evidence received from retail customers" — so evidence requests and re-statement loops are measured as part of the path rather than treated as outside it.

**Because the path is generated, one walk-through is a sample.** Each walk-through is repeated at a pinned configuration — model version, prompt build, guardrail and journey configuration all fixed — and the reported figure is the distribution across runs, not a single observation. This applies the repeat-run discipline IRQ-MOD-014 establishes for output stability, for the same reason: a system that produces a short exit path on one run and a long one on the next has not passed, and a single run cannot tell the difference.

**Limb (b) — paired near-identical-profile probes.** Construct matched synthetic profiles differing only on the attribute suspected of driving personalisation, run each pair down the same exit path at the same configuration, and compare step count, turn count, default pre-selection and whether a retention offer was invoked. **Divergence is reported pair by pair.** An averaged asymmetry is the wrong statistic here: the question is whether this customer met a longer path than that one, and aggregation conceals exactly the case the limb exists to find. The conceptual basis is the individual-treatment principle at Dwork et al. (2012) §1.1 — similar individuals should be treated similarly — and the limb is otherwise a first-principles construction with no published validation, which the report states.

**Limb (c) — retention-dialogue and personalisation configuration review.** Read the layer's system prompt, policy and guardrail configuration, its objective or reward signal, any retention or save instruction, and any variant-selection, propensity-routing or experiment configuration. Assess each against FG22/5 ¶9.27: retention prompts and incentives are acceptable, and the bound is that they should not unreasonably impact the ease of switching or exiting. The question is whether that bound exists anywhere the system can act on it, or only in a policy the layer never reads.

**Classification is the judgement and it is recorded as one.** Every friction point found in any limb is classified against PRIN 2A.6.3G as appropriate friction — included to mitigate harm and give the customer opportunity to understand and assess their options, which 2A.6.3G(2) provides does not amount to an unreasonable barrier — or as an unreasonable barrier, against 2A.6.3G(1)'s gloss. **No threshold is fixed by FAIAS and none exists in the field.** The reviewer records the classification basis for each point before the aggregate conclusion is drawn, and the report states that the materiality judgement was the reviewer's.

## 4. Steps
1. Inventory every customer-journey path the AI layer mediates and map each to the PRIN 2A.6.2R(4)(a)–(g) actions, identifying the matched acquisition or upgrade path for each. **The rule's list is illustrative, not exhaustive** — 2A.6.2R(4) prohibits unreasonable barriers "during the lifecycle of a product, such as when they want to" the seven listed actions — so a lifecycle action the deployment supports that is not one of the seven is mapped as a further in-scope action, not excluded because the rule does not name it. **Record:** the inventory, the mapping including any such further action, and any lifecycle action the deployment supports but that is excluded from scope, with the reason.
2. Obtain any existing sludge-audit report. **Record:** its date, scope, whether it examined the journey as designed or as generated, and whether the AI layer existed when it was performed — this is the evidence for or against the blind-spot finding and is not an optional preliminary.
3. Obtain the layer's objective, reward or optimisation target and any retention, save or containment instruction, and identify who owns it. **Record:** the target as configured, the owner, or that no owner holds it.
4. Verify configuration parity between the test or staging instance and the intended production release, including retention and save logic. **Record:** version identifiers for model, prompt build, guardrail and journey configuration in both environments.
5. Fix the measurement set and the classification rubric **before any walk-through**: which measures are recorded, how a step and a turn are counted, how many repeat runs per path, and the basis on which a friction point will be classified appropriate or unreasonable. **Record:** the set, the rubric and the timestamp. Classifying friction points after seeing the asymmetry is the same failure as post-hoc metric selection in TP-CON-001 and is just as fatal.
6. Walk each in-scope exit path with the repeat-run protocol at the pinned configuration. **Record:** per run and per path — steps, screens, turns, mandatory fields, pre-selected options, elapsed time; and the full dialogue by identifier in the evidence store.
7. Walk each matched acquisition or upgrade path with the same protocol. **Record:** the same measures.
8. Compute the comparison per action: exit against acquisition, as a distribution across runs. **Record:** the comparison, and against FG22/5 ¶9.26's standard, whether switching out is at least as easy as buying in.
9. Construct the matched profile pairs and run limb (b). **Record:** pair by pair — the attribute varied, the divergence on each measure, and whether a retention offer was invoked for one member and not the other.
10. Run limb (c) on the configuration. **Record:** the retention instruction as configured, whether any bound on it exists in configuration, and every mechanism by which the journey varies per customer — or the firm's inability to say whether it varies, which is itself the finding.
11. Classify every friction point found against PRIN 2A.6.3G(1)–(2), independently by two reviewers, with disagreements adjudicated. **Record:** the classification, the basis, the disagreement rate and a chance-corrected agreement statistic (Cohen's kappa for two reviewers, per ASSURANCE_MODEL.md §7A; no threshold is prescribed).
12. Review production data: completion and abandonment by path against the acquisition baseline, retention-offer acceptance on exit journeys, and complaint themes on difficulty of cancelling, switching, claiming or complaining. **Record:** any pattern, and whether it corresponds to anything the walk-throughs found.
13. For each material asymmetry, establish whether it has been documented and either formally accepted with a stated basis by the accountable owner or remediated. **Record:** the acceptance or the absence of one.

## 6. Interpretation (per CANVASES_CON.md fields 12–14)
**Pass** where the exit-versus-acquisition comparison has been performed for every PRIN 2A.6.2R(4) action the deployment supports, with repeat runs rather than a single walk-through; no material asymmetry is found, or each is classified, documented and either accepted with a stated basis or remediated; paired probes show no unexplained divergence between matched profiles; the FG22/5 ¶9.27 retention bound is operative in configuration; and any per-customer personalisation of friction is inventoried and justified.

**Fail** where: no exit-versus-acquisition comparison exists and the journey's conduct evidence is a sludge audit of the journey as designed; **or** a material asymmetry is found and is unclassified, undocumented and unaccepted; **or** paired probes show friction varying with a customer attribute with no recorded justification, or the firm cannot say whether its journey varies friction per customer; **or** the layer carries a retention, save or containment objective with no bound on it in configuration; **or** production data shows exit-path abandonment materially above the acquisition baseline, or complaint themes on difficulty of exit, with no corresponding finding in prior testing.

**Partial** where — the canvas's own shape — the comparison was performed for the cancellation path and found no material asymmetry, but the claim and complaint paths, both named in PRIN 2A.6.2R(4), were not measured and no paired probe was run. Also partial where an irreversible downstream action prevented completion of a path in staging and the remaining steps were assessed on artefacts: say which paths were walked and which were read.

- Friction-point register with each point classified against PRIN 2A.6.3G and the basis recorded — **L2**

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Limitations
- **Accepting a sludge audit as coverage.** This is the canvas's central blind-spot finding and the reason the IRQ exists. FG22/5 ¶11.24 places sludge audits among activities carried out "at regular intervals or on an ad hoc basis" — a statement about **frequency only**: "sludge audits" appears once in the whole of FG22/5 and is never scoped, so that such an audit examines the journey *as designed* is this standard's inference and not the FCA's. On that inference, a layer that composes the journey at run time is outside what the audit observed. Step 2 exists to make the distinction evidenced rather than argued.
- **Walking each path once.** The friction is generated, so a single walk-through measures one sample of the system's behaviour. Without repeat runs at a pinned configuration, a short exit path on the observed run and a long one on every other run are indistinguishable.
- **Measuring cancellation only.** PRIN 2A.6.2R(4) names seven actions — illustratively, the rule reading "such as when they want to" — and submitting a claim and making a complaint are among them. Cancellation-only coverage is the canvas's partial condition, and treating the seven as a closed list under-scopes the rule.
- **Classifying friction points after seeing the asymmetry.** Post-hoc classification is this domain's most serious available methodological failure, exactly as post-hoc fairness-metric selection is in TP-CON-001, and it is easier to commit here because every individual step has a defensible protective reading available.
- **Treating protective friction as a finding.** PRIN 2A.6.3G(2) is explicit that appropriate friction included to comply with 2A.6.2R(3) does not amount to an unreasonable barrier. A confirmation step that gives the customer opportunity to understand and assess their options is what the rule requires, not a breach of it. The test is asymmetry and purpose, not step count alone.
- **Averaging the paired probes.** Aggregation conceals the individual divergence the limb exists to find. Report pair by pair.
- **Citing Dwork et al. as a method.** Definition 2.1 is a Lipschitz constraint on a classifier's output distribution. The paired-profile limb borrows the §1.1 principle and nothing more; presenting it as an application of a published fairness procedure would overstate the method's standing, which is the ★1 rating's whole content.
- **Reading the ★1 as "the harm is speculative".** It is not: PRIN 2A.6.2R(4) is a binding rule. The rating records that the *method* is novel and unvalidated and that materiality is reviewer-set. Reports that collapse the two mislead in the firm's favour.
- **Completing an irreversible action to finish a path.** A staging cancellation that closes a real account, or a complaint that enters a real complaints queue, breaches the standing non-production rule. Stop at the last reversible step, assess the remainder on artefacts, and record the substitution.
- **Omitting the objective's owner.** Where no one holds the layer's retention or containment target, there is no one to accept the asymmetry or to bound the retention dialogue. That absence is a governance finding and cross-links to IRQ-GOV-003 and IRQ-MOD-007, not a gap in this procedure.
- **Regulatory-basis limitation carried forward.** Beyond the Consumer Duty rule and guidance the canvas cites, no FCA or PRA publication addressing AI-generated or dynamically personalised journey friction specifically was located. Do not present the conclusion as measured against a regulator-defined AI standard; there is none. EU Digital Fairness Act / DSA and CMA dark-patterns work were not checked.

## 9. Time / Effort Estimate
**5 reviewer-days**: 0.5 day journey inventory, lifecycle-action mapping and sludge-audit scope review; 0.5 day objective, ownership and configuration-parity work; 0.5 day pre-registration of measures, counting conventions and classification rubric; 1.5 days walk-throughs with repeat runs across exit and matched acquisition paths (the cost driver — cost scales with the number of in-scope lifecycle actions multiplied by the repeat-run count, not with design time); 1 day paired-profile construction, execution and pair-by-pair reporting; 0.5 day configuration and personalisation review; 0.5 day production-data and acceptance limbs, dual classification adjudication and working papers. Second-reviewer classification time is included. Add 1 day for each additional product line whose acquisition path must be separately matched, and 0.5 day where a path cannot be completed in staging and must be assessed on artefacts.
