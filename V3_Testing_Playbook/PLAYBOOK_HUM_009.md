# TP-HUM-009 — Evidential Standard for AI-Triggered Adverse Allegations: Case-File and Disclosure Review

**IRQ:** IRQ-HUM-009 — Evidential Standard for AI-Triggered Adverse Allegations (subdomain D.4).
**Assurance objective:** AO-HUM-009 — where AI output initiates or materially contributes to an adverse allegation, decision or sanction against a customer or colleague, the institution shall apply a documented, proportionate evidential-sufficiency threshold before acting, disclose the underlying evidence and reasoning to the subject as a matter of course, and shall not require the subject to disprove the AI output before the adverse action is reversed or suspended.
**Confidence:** ★1 (carried unchanged). Method class: **None established** — no published testing methodology exists for evidential sufficiency of an AI-originated allegation as a discipline, and the canvas states this plainly rather than implying it. This is a professional-judgement documentation and case-file review, not a quantitative or benchmarked test. The canvas's basis for the rating: **the harm mechanism is strongly evidenced across independent jurisdictions, but there is no established review methodology and no comparable published benchmark for what a proportionate evidential standard means for AI output specifically; judgement dominates entirely.** Two of the five expected controls — the evidential-sufficiency threshold itself, and periodic case-file sampling against it — are marked Emerging Practice in the canvas's control list.

### Preconditions
- **Applicability.** In scope where AI output initiates or materially contributes to an adverse allegation, decision or sanction against a customer or colleague — fraud and financial-crime case management; credit and account decisioning framed as suspected misconduct rather than ordinary credit risk; and colleague conduct or surveillance-driven investigations.
- **No live adverse action is affected by this procedure at any point.** Case files are real historical files that have been de-identified, or dev/test equivalents. Nothing is re-decided, delayed, reversed or influenced as a consequence of the review.
- Read access to: the documented evidential-standard policy, if one exists, and specifically its stated relationship to the general complaints-handling framework (FCA DISP 1.1.3R); the adverse-action workflow design, including what the workflow generates and sends to the subject and when; a drawable population of adverse-action case files initiated or materially driven by AI output, spanning every in-scope decision type; the case-file sampling cycle documentation where recurring review is claimed; production complaint or appeal outcome data touching AI-originated adverse actions, where held.
- Where the review includes interviews with investigating managers or case handlers, the domain's research-participant discipline applies: informed consent covering purpose and data use, the sponsoring executive's written non-punitive assurance, and individual-level results reported only in aggregate. **No blinding and no deception are required by this design, and no debrief obligation arises from them.**
- Data handling: adverse-action case files concern identified individuals and frequently record allegations of misconduct. Handle under engagement confidentiality, restrict to the review team, obtain data-protection clearance for the de-identification route, and report by decision type and case reference rather than by name in any wider circulation.
- Personnel: lead reviewer; second reviewer for dual scoring of the sufficiency and disclosure determinations; the owner of the adverse-action process (fraud, conduct or credit investigation as applicable); where colleague conduct cases are in scope, the function that runs them.
- **Dependency:** run after or alongside TP-HUM-008 where both are in scope. The canvas is explicit that the contest route tested there is meaningless without the disclosure this procedure tests — a subject who has never been shown the basis of an allegation has nothing concrete to contest.

### Test design
**A documentation review and a case-file sample, scored against three questions, and nothing more.** The canvas defines the test as exactly this; there is no behavioural limb and none is constructed.

**Documentation limb.** Establish whether a documented evidential-sufficiency threshold exists, whether it is proportionate to consequence severity, and — critically — whether it is documented as **distinct from and additional to the general complaints-handling framework**. The canvas records the conflation of the two as a fail indicator in its own right: DISP 1.1.3R presupposes a decision has already been made and communicated and gives the subject a route to complain about it; it does not itself set an evidential-sufficiency threshold for the AI-originated allegation feeding that decision, so compliance with the complaints-handling framework alone does not satisfy this control.

**Case-file limb.** For each sampled case, three questions are answered, and they are independent of one another and of whether the outcome was correct:
1. Was a defined evidential-sufficiency threshold, proportionate to the severity of the consequence, **applied and met** before the adverse action was taken?
2. Were the underlying evidence and reasoning **disclosed to the subject at or before the point of action** — as a matter of course, rather than on request or after complaint?
3. Did the process require the **subject to disprove** the allegation before the action was reversed or suspended, rather than requiring the institution to justify it?

**Scoring discipline.** The rubric for all three is fixed and timestamped before any file is opened, and both reviewers score independently with disagreements adjudicated and the adjudication recorded. Standard case-quality audit checks whether the final decision was correct; that is a different question, and a clean outcome record is not evidence here.

**Sampling.** Risk-based, spanning every decision type in scope — the canvas's partial condition is precisely a standard applied to one decision type and not another. Where colleague conduct cases exist alongside customer cases, both are sampled; the canvas names colleague investigations as a distinct affected activity. The canvas prescribes no sample size, so the size and its rationale are documented as reviewer judgement.

**Recurrence limb.** Establish whether case-file sampling against the evidential standard is performed on a recurring cycle by the firm itself, or has never been performed at all. The canvas records "the control is asserted, not evidenced" as a fail indicator separate from any individual case failing.

### Steps
1. Confirm applicability and identify every decision type in which AI output initiates or materially contributes to an adverse allegation. **Record:** the inventory — it defines the sampling frame and the scope of any partial finding.
2. Request the documented evidential-sufficiency threshold and disclosure policy. **Record:** whether it exists, whether it scales to consequence severity, and whether it is documented as distinct from the general complaints-handling framework or relies on it.
3. Fix and timestamp the scoring rubric for the three case-file questions before any file is opened.
4. Agree and record the case-file sample, spanning all in-scope decision types and including colleague conduct cases where they exist. **Record:** the sampling basis, sizes and the de-identification route.
5. Walk through the adverse-action workflow design with the process owner and establish **what the workflow generates and sends to the subject, and at what point**. **Record:** whether a statement of the underlying evidence and reasoning is produced at or before action, on request only, after complaint, or not at all.
6. Score each sampled case against question 1 — threshold applied and met before action. **Record:** the evidence relied on in each case, and where the AI flag alone was the basis.
7. Score each sampled case against question 2 — disclosure at or before the point of action. **Record:** the artefact disclosed, or that disclosure occurred only on request, after complaint, or not at all.
8. Score each sampled case against question 3 — burden. **Record:** any case in which the subject was required to disprove the allegation before the action was reversed or suspended.
9. Both reviewers score independently; adjudicate disagreements. **Record:** the adjudication and the agreement rate.
10. Test the framework-distinctness limb: does any document assert that complaints-framework compliance satisfies the evidential-standard requirement? **Record:** the assertion, if found, as a fail input.
11. Establish whether the firm performs recurring case-file sampling against the evidential standard. **Record:** the cycle, the last execution, findings and remediation, or its complete absence.
12. Review production complaint or appeal outcome data for whether subjects were able meaningfully to contest allegations whose basis they had been shown. **Record:** any such data, or that none was identified.

### Observations & measures
- **Evidential-sufficiency threshold** (categorical): documented, proportionate to consequence severity, and distinct from the complaints-handling framework / documented but not distinct, or not scaled to severity / none exists. Canvas fail indicators cover the second and third; the second is the specific conflation the canvas warns against.
- **Threshold met in sampled cases** (per case): defined, proportionate threshold applied and met / defined but not met before action / no defined threshold. Canvas pass indicator requires the sampled files to meet it. **No established threshold for the proportion — reviewer judgement**, basis recorded.
- **Proactive disclosure rate** = cases where the underlying evidence and reasoning were disclosed at or before the point of action ÷ cases sampled. Canvas pass indicator: **the large majority**, disclosed as a matter of course rather than on request or after complaint. Not numerically defined in any cited source — **reviewer judgement**, with the interval reported given likely small samples.
- **Burden position** (binary, per case): the institution justifies the action / the subject bore the practical burden of disproving the AI-originated allegation before the action was reversed or suspended. Canvas pass indicator: **no sampled case** shows the burden reversed. A single case is a finding.
- **Framework distinctness** (binary): the evidential-standard policy is documented as distinct from, and additional to, the general complaints-handling framework / the firm treats complaints-handling compliance as if it independently satisfied the requirement. Canvas fail indicator: the second.
- **Recurrence** (categorical): case-file sampling against the evidential standard is repeated on a recurring cycle / performed once / never performed. Canvas fail indicator: never performed — the control is asserted, not evidenced.
- **Dual-scoring agreement** — reported alongside every proportion above, because all three questions are judgement calls and the ★1 rating turns on that.

### Interpretation
- **Pass** — a documented evidential-sufficiency threshold exists, proportionate to consequence severity, and the sampled case files meet it; disclosure of the evidence and reasoning occurred at or before the point of action for the large majority of sampled cases, not only on request or after complaint; no sampled case shows the subject bearing the burden of disproving the AI-originated allegation before the action was reversed or suspended; the policy is documented as distinct from and additional to the general complaints-handling framework; and case-file sampling against the standard is repeated on a recurring cycle rather than treated as a one-off validation.
- **Fail** — no documented evidential-sufficiency threshold exists and adverse action proceeds on AI output alone with no defined basis; **or** disclosure of the evidence and reasoning occurs only on request, after complaint, or not at all; **or** sampled cases show the subject bore the practical burden of disproving the allegation; **or** the firm treats compliance with the general complaints-handling framework as if it independently satisfied the evidential-standard requirement; **or** no case-file sampling or documentation review against an evidential standard has ever been performed — the control is asserted, not evidenced.
- **Partial** — the canvas's own shape: the threshold is applied and disclosure occurs for one decision type, while another relies on the general complaints-handling framework alone with no distinct evidential-standard policy and no proactive disclosure. Remediation should extend the evidential-standard control to the uncovered decision type before continued reliance. Name the decision types; the finding is unusable without them.

### Evidence outputs
- E1 Policy assertion that adverse actions are reviewed by a human before being actioned — **L1**.
- E2 Documented evidential-sufficiency threshold and disclosure policy, distinct from the general complaints-handling framework — **L2**.
- E3 Pre-registered scoring rubric for the three case-file questions, timestamped before any file was opened — **L3**.
- E4 Sampling record with basis, sizes, decision-type coverage and de-identification route, including colleague conduct cases where in scope — **L3**.
- E5 Design or process walkthrough confirming disclosure of the evidence and reasoning is generated at or before the point of action, not only on request — **L3**.
- E6 Case-file scoring sheets across the three questions, with the evidence relied on recorded per case — **L3**.
- E7 Dual-scoring adjudication record and agreement rate — **L3**.
- E8 Independent audit tracing a sample of real historical adverse-action case files to confirm the evidential threshold was applied, disclosure occurred, and the burden was not reversed onto the subject — **L4**.
- E9 Production complaint or appeal outcome data indicating whether subjects were able meaningfully to contest allegations they had been shown the basis for — **L5**.

### Common pitfalls & limitations
- **Scoring outcome correctness instead.** Standard case-quality audit asks whether the decision was right. This procedure asks whether the evidential standard was met and whether the subject was shown the basis. A file with a correct outcome can fail all three questions.
- **Accepting a human-review step as the control.** "A human reviews and actions the flag" says nothing about the evidential standard that human applies. A review step without a defined threshold is not the control this procedure tests for.
- **Accepting complaints-handling compliance as the answer.** DISP 1.1.3R binds the firm once a complaint arrives. It sets no evidential threshold for the allegation that produced the decision being complained about, and the canvas makes the conflation an explicit fail indicator.
- **Confusing disclosure on request with disclosure as a matter of course.** The canvas's control is disclosure at or before the point of action. A route to obtain the reasoning afterwards is a different control, and it is the one most firms actually have.
- **Sampling customer cases only.** Colleague conduct and surveillance-driven investigations are a named affected activity, and are where an AI flag is most likely to be treated by a manager as sufficient grounds on its own.
- **Setting the rubric after reading files.** The same failure mode as post-hoc metric selection. Step 3 precedes step 4 for that reason.
- **Small samples reported with false precision.** Adverse-action populations for a given AI flag type are often small. Report counts and intervals, and say plainly where the study is underpowered.
- **Treating an absence of complaints as evidence.** A subject who was never shown the basis of an allegation has no material with which to complain. Low complaint volume on an undisclosed process is consistent with the failure mode, not against it.
- **Letting the test touch a live case.** Executed entirely on de-identified or dev/test case files; no live adverse action is affected.
- **★1 honesty.** The harm mechanism is strongly evidenced — the Robodebt Royal Commission (Report Vol. 1, 2023), the Michigan MiDAS system, the UK Home Office/ETS TOEIC programme per the National Audit Office's May 2019 investigation, the Post Office Horizon system per the Inquiry's Volume 1 report of July 2025 (an automation analogue), and the Dutch childcare benefits risk-classification model (on a non-governmental organisation's report): five independent systems in four jurisdictions, the same failure shape — but there is no established review methodology and no benchmark for what a proportionate evidential standard means for AI output specifically. Judgement dominates entirely. Report as expert opinion, not evidence, and state that the conclusion should be re-sampled on a recurring cycle rather than relied on as durable.

### Time/effort estimate
**4.0 reviewer-days** — 0.5 applicability, decision-type inventory and policy review including the framework-distinctness assessment; 0.5 rubric fixing, sampling design and de-identification coordination; 0.5 workflow walkthrough establishing what is disclosed and when; 1.5 case-file scoring across the three questions (the dominant cost; scales with sample size and decision-type count); 0.5 dual-scoring adjudication; 0.5 recurrence limb, complaint/appeal data review and write-up. Firm-side effort for file retrieval and de-identification is additional and is frequently the critical path; this procedure adds no firm-side participant days.
