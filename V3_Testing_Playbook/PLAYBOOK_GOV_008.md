# TP-GOV-008 — Escalation Path Reconciliation

## 1. Procedure Identity
**Procedure ID:** TP-GOV-008
**IRQ:** IRQ-GOV-008 — Operational Monitoring Signals Not Reaching Accountable Governance Bodies
**Assurance objective:** AO-GOV-008 — Monitoring results, incidents and drift signals for deployed AI systems shall flow to a named accountable governance body through a defined escalation trigger, with documented authority and evidenced practice of acting on signals that meet threshold.
**★ Confidence (carried from canvas, unchanged):** ★3 — credible, established escalation-path reconciliation methodology adapted from operational resilience testing practice; no AI-specific standard defines acceptable escalation latency or governance review adequacy for AI signals, so professional judgement determines whether the traced path constitutes effective escalation.
**Method classification:** Adapted Industry Practice — escalation-path reconciliation and control testing as applied to operational resilience incident escalation.

## 2. Preconditions
**Environment:** Governance records, committee minutes and interviews. No production system access required.
**Access required:** Read access to the monitoring alert, drift-breach and incident records generated by the operational monitoring layer (the RES G.3 output tested in TP-RES-005); governance committee terms of reference and minutes; governance decision logs; access to committee members for interview.
**Artefacts required:** Alert/breach/incident records for the review period across sampled systems; escalation trigger definitions and any escalation SLA; committee terms of reference; committee minutes and packs; governance decision log; records of revalidation, restriction or withdrawal decisions.
**Personnel required:** Monitoring function; committee secretary; governance committee members (minimum three interviews — a reviewer-set design minimum, disclosed at scoping); reviewer.
**Data handling:** Committee minutes are confidential and may be partly privileged. Review in situ; record findings by reference.

## 3. Test Design
Forward tracing from signal to decision, over a sample of historical signals. For each sampled signal the reviewer establishes four facts: did an escalation trigger fire; which governance body received it; how long between signal generation and governance review; and what decision, if any, was made.

This procedure is the governance-consumption counterpart to TP-RES-005, which tests whether the signal was generated and routed. Run TP-RES-005 first; its output supplies the signal population for this sample. Do not double-report a broken link.

Two corroborating limbs: a documentary check that AI operational signals appear as a standing item in committee terms of reference and minutes, and interviews testing whether members actually recall reviewing AI monitoring output.

Sampling: draw across signal types (alert, drift-threshold breach, incident) and across systems, weighted to Tier 2–3. Minimum 15 signals; include at least three of the most severe signals in the period (reviewer-set design minimum, disclosed at scoping), since severity should correlate with escalation and frequently does not.

## 4. Steps
1. Obtain the signal population from the monitoring layer for the review period. **Record:** total counts by type and system.
2. Draw and record the sample. **Record:** basis, size, type and severity spread.
3. Obtain the escalation trigger definitions and any escalation SLA. **Record:** triggers; the SLA value; whether an SLA exists at all.
4. For each sampled signal, trace: whether an escalation trigger fired. **Record:** fired / did not fire / no applicable trigger defined.
5. For each signal where a trigger fired, identify which governance body received it. **Record:** body; route; whether the receiving body was the one named in the escalation design.
6. Measure elapsed time from signal generation to governance-level review. **Record:** per-signal latency; comparison against the firm's own SLA.
7. Identify the decision made at governance level. **Record:** decision type — noted / investigated / revalidation ordered / restriction imposed / withdrawal — per signal. The distribution across these categories is the procedure's headline finding.
8. Review committee terms of reference. **Record:** whether AI operational signals are a defined standing item.
9. Review committee minutes for the period. **Record:** every reference to AI operational monitoring output; and, where the signal population shows qualifying alerts occurred, whether minutes reflect them.
10. Review the governance decision log. **Record:** at least one instance of revalidation, restriction or withdrawal taken in direct response to an escalated signal — or its absence.
11. Interview committee members (minimum three). Ask them to describe recent AI monitoring signals they reviewed and the decisions taken. **Record:** verbatim; whether members are aware AI-specific signals should reach them at all.
12. **Cross-deployment check.** Where a shared component (a common model version, a shared index) serves multiple deployments, test whether a drift signal on one deployment escalated to the others. **Record:** any instance found or its absence.

## 5. Observations & Measures
| Measure | Threshold | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Sampled signals traceable to governance review | 100%, within the firm's own defined SLA | Categorical per canvas pass indicator; the SLA is firm-set |
| Escalation latency | The firm's own SLA. No AI-specific standard defines acceptable latency | Per canvas; explicitly reviewer judgement where no SLA exists |
| Governance decisions beyond "noted" | ≥1 revalidation, restriction or withdrawal in the period | Categorical per canvas fail indicator ("only 'noted' entries") |
| AI signals as a standing committee item | Present in terms of reference and evidenced in minutes | Categorical per canvas pass indicator |
| Member recall of specific signals and decisions | Members can describe recent signals reviewed | Categorical per canvas pass indicator |
| Cross-deployment escalation for shared components | ≥1 instance where applicable | Per canvas pass indicator |

## 6. Interpretation (per CANVASES_GOV.md fields 12–14)
**Pass** where: 100% of sampled signals are traceable to governance review within the firm's SLA; terms of reference and minutes confirm AI operational signals are a standing actively-reviewed item; the decision log shows at least one revalidation, restriction or withdrawal in direct response to an escalated signal; members can describe recent signals and decisions; and cross-deployment escalation is evidenced for shared components where applicable.

**Fail** where: sampled signals show no trace of governance review (the path exists in policy but not practice); minutes contain no reference to AI operational signals despite qualifying alerts having occurred; the decision log shows only "noted" entries with no substantive decisions ever taken; latency materially exceeds the firm's own SLA with no compensating control; or members are unaware that AI-specific signals exist or should reach them.

**Partial** where incidents escalate reliably but routine drift-threshold breaches do not — the common pattern, since incident escalation is a mature process and drift escalation is new.

## 7. Evidence Outputs
- Escalation trigger definitions and SLA — **L2**
- Committee terms of reference and minutes (by reference where confidential) — **L2**
- Signal-to-decision trace matrix with latencies — **L3**
- Decision-type distribution analysis — **L3**
- Reviewer's independent escalation-effectiveness assessment — **L4**
- Governance decision records evidencing real actions taken on signals — **L5**

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Limitations
- **Sampling only escalated signals.** The population must be all signals, so that non-escalated ones can be found. Sampling from the escalation log guarantees a 100% pass.
- **Counting "noted" as consumption.** The canvas is explicit: a log of noted entries with no substantive decision is a fail indicator.
- **Measuring latency to receipt rather than to review.** A signal sitting in a pack unreviewed for six weeks has not escalated.
- **Overlap with TP-RES-005.** RES-005 ends at signal generation and routing; this procedure begins there. Cross-reference; do not double-count the same broken link as two findings.
- **The shared-component check is easy to skip and frequently the most valuable.** A drift signal that escalated for one deployment and not for four others using the same model is a systemic finding.

## 9. Time / Effort Estimate
**4 reviewer-days**: 0.5 day population extraction and sampling, 1.5 days signal-to-decision tracing, 1 day minutes and decision log review, 0.5 day interviews, 0.5 day working papers.
