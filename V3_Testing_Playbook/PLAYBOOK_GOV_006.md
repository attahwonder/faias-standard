# TP-GOV-006 — Risk Appetite Measurability and Application Test

## 1. Procedure Identity
**Procedure ID:** TP-GOV-006
**IRQ:** IRQ-GOV-006 — Absent or Unmeasurable Board-Approved AI Risk Appetite
**Assurance objective:** AO-GOV-006 — The board shall approve an AI-specific risk appetite statement with measurable indicators, and individual deployment decisions shall be demonstrably assessed against it.
**★ Confidence (carried from canvas, unchanged):** ★2 — regulatory expectation that board-level AI accountability exists is well-established, but no published methodology or benchmark defines what a "measurable" AI risk appetite statement must contain; the rubric and adequacy judgement are bespoke and rely heavily on reviewer professional judgement, informed by comparison to the firm's own traditional risk-appetite frameworks.
**Method classification:** Adapted Industry Practice (board-effectiveness and risk-appetite-framework review) extended by an Emerging Practice rubric.

## 2. Preconditions
**Environment:** Board papers, approval records and interviews. No system access.
**Access required:** The board-approved AI risk appetite statement (if any); the firm's credit, market and operational risk appetite statements as the internal comparison standard; deployment approval papers across risk tiers; board and risk committee reporting packs; access to board/risk committee members for interview.
**Artefacts required:** AI risk appetite statement with approval date and approving body; comparator risk appetite statements; a sample of deployment approval papers spanning tiers; appetite reporting/MI over the review period; records of any deployment decision shaped by an appetite threshold.
**Personnel required:** Chief Risk Officer or risk framework owner; board or risk committee members (minimum three interviews — a reviewer-set design minimum, disclosed at scoping); reviewer.
**Data handling:** Board papers are highly confidential and frequently legally privileged in part. Review in situ under the firm's arrangements; do not export. Record findings by reference, not by extract, where privilege is asserted.

## 3. Test Design
The procedure answers three questions in sequence:

- **Does a statement exist, and is it measurable?** Assessed against a rubric derived from how the firm constructs its *own* traditional risk appetite statements. This internal comparison is the methodological core: it avoids the reviewer imposing an external standard that does not exist, and it produces a finding the board cannot easily dismiss ("your AI appetite is qualitative where your operational risk appetite is quantified").
- **Is it applied?** Do deployment approval papers actually reference and test against the indicators, or is approval reasoning silent on appetite?
- **Is it live in the boardroom?** Can board and risk committee members describe current AI risk exposure relative to appetite?

The rubric is fixed and recorded before assessment. Dimensions drawn from the comparator statements typically include: quantified or otherwise measurable indicators; defined thresholds or limits; a named metric owner; a reporting cadence; and defined escalation on breach. Record the rubric's derivation explicitly — the canvas rates this ★2 largely because the rubric is bespoke.

## 4. Steps
1. Obtain the AI risk appetite statement. **Record:** existence; approval date; approving body; date of last review. Where none exists, that is the primary finding and steps 5–9 still run to establish what fills the gap.
2. Obtain the comparator risk appetite statements (credit, market, operational). **Record:** their structural features — indicators, thresholds, owners, cadence, escalation.
3. Construct and record the assessment rubric from step 2's structural features. **Record:** the rubric and its derivation, before assessing the AI statement.
4. Assess the AI statement against the rubric. **Record:** dimension-by-dimension score; specifically whether indicators are measurable or solely qualitative/aspirational.
5. Draw a sample of deployment approval papers across risk tiers. **Record:** sample basis and tier spread.
6. For each approval paper, test whether it explicitly references the appetite indicators and assesses the deployment against them. **Record:** references / mentions without assessment / silent.
7. Search for at least one deployment decision demonstrably shaped by an appetite threshold — an approval conditioned, a restriction imposed, or a decline. **Record:** the instance or its absence. This is the strongest single evidence that the appetite operates.
8. Review board and risk committee reporting. **Record:** whether appetite indicators are tracked over time against actual exposure; the cadence; whether aggregate AI exposure is reported at all.
9. Interview board/risk committee members (minimum three). Ask them to describe the current level of AI risk exposure relative to appetite. **Record:** verbatim responses; consistency across interviewees.
10. Assess consistency. **Record:** whether members give broadly consistent accounts, materially inconsistent accounts, or cannot describe exposure relative to any defined appetite.
11. Review appetite currency against estate growth. **Record:** the AI estate size at appetite approval versus now; whether the appetite has been revisited.

## 5. Observations & Measures
| Measure | Threshold | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Statement exists with measurable indicators | Present; a generic "responsible AI" values statement with no measurable indicators is a fail indicator | Categorical per canvas |
| Rubric dimensions met | Reviewer judgement against the firm's own comparator statements — no established threshold. No published methodology defines what a measurable AI appetite must contain | Explicitly bespoke; the reason for ★2 |
| Approval papers referencing and testing against appetite | All sampled papers across tiers | Categorical per canvas pass indicator |
| Deployment decisions shaped by an appetite threshold | ≥1 evidenced instance | Categorical per canvas pass indicator |
| Board member consistency on current exposure vs appetite | Broadly consistent accounts across interviewees | Categorical per canvas — materially inconsistent answers are a fail indicator |
| Appetite reviewed against estate growth | Reviewer judgement — approval once with no revisit despite significant growth is a fail indicator | Per canvas |

## 6. Interpretation (per CANVASES_GOV.md fields 12–14)
**Pass** where: a board-approved AI risk appetite statement exists with defined measurable indicators; a sample of approval papers across tiers explicitly references and tests against them; board/risk committee reporting tracks the indicators against actual exposure over time; at least one deployment decision is evidenced as having been shaped by an appetite threshold; and interviewed members describe current exposure relative to appetite in broadly consistent terms.

**Fail** where: no statement exists or the only statement is a generic values statement with no measurable indicators; approval papers make no reference to appetite; no reporting tracks aggregate exposure against appetite; members cannot describe exposure relative to any defined appetite or give materially inconsistent answers; or the statement was approved once and never revisited despite significant estate growth.

**Partial** where a measurable statement exists and is reported, but approval papers do not test against it — appetite as a monitoring artefact rather than a decision gate.

## 7. Evidence Outputs
- Appetite statement and comparator statements as reviewed (by reference where privileged) — **L2**
- Rubric with its derivation, recorded pre-assessment — **L2**
- Rubric scoring of the AI statement — **L3**
- Approval paper testing results across the tier sample — **L3**
- Reviewer's independent measurability and application assessment — **L4**
- Board reporting showing exposure tracked against appetite over time — **L5**

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Limitations
- **Importing an external standard.** None exists. The comparator-based rubric is what makes the finding defensible; say so.
- **Interviewing only the CRO.** The CRO will describe the appetite accurately. Non-executive members' answers are the test of whether it is live.
- **Accepting "we discuss AI risk at every meeting".** Discussion is not appetite. Step 7 looks for a decision the appetite actually shaped.
- **Board paper privilege.** Plan the access arrangements early; a late privilege objection can strand the procedure mid-fieldwork.
- **Three interviews is a small base.** Report consistency qualitatively, not as a percentage.

## 9. Time / Effort Estimate
**3 reviewer-days**: 0.5 day statement and comparator review, 0.5 day rubric construction, 1 day approval paper testing, 0.5 day board member interviews, 0.5 day working papers. Elapsed time is typically longer than effort because board member availability governs scheduling.
