# TP-TPR-009 — AI Vendor Financial-Standing and Continuity-on-Failure Review

**IRQ:** IRQ-TPR-009 — Vendor Financial Standing and Continuity on Vendor Failure (subdomain F.1).
**Assurance objective:** AO-TPR-009 — the firm shall assess the financial standing, funding runway and management integrity of AI vendors, proportionate to the materiality of the dependent business activity, in addition to safety, security and model-weakness diligence, and shall secure the ability to retrieve its data, models, prompts and configurations and continue the dependent service, in-house or via a substitute provider, if the vendor fails or exits mid-contract.
**Confidence:** ★1 (carried unchanged). Method class: **None established** — the canvas states in terms that **no published testing methodology exists for this discipline; this is a due diligence and contract review item, described as such, not an empirical test.** The underlying discipline the review applies is Emerging Practice: general outsourcing and vendor-risk due diligence (EBA/GL/2019/02, Title IV, Section 12, Pre-outsourcing analysis, including 12.3 Due diligence, with criticality determined under Title II, Section 4, Critical or important functions; PRA SS2/21, March 2021; DORA Arts. 28–30 for the contractual limb) extended to the AI vendor population, with the AI-vendor financial-standing diligence checklist itself marked Emerging Practice in the canvas's control list. **No published FS-specific standard names AI vendor financial-standing diligence as a distinct discipline**, and the canvas records the mapping from the general outsourcing guidelines as an interpretive extension, not a directly AI-named requirement. The canvas's basis for the rating: the mechanism — commercial failure risk sitting outside safety and security diligence — is well understood in general vendor risk management, but the AI-vendor-specific incident evidence base is thin, and no FS-specific benchmark or acceptance threshold exists for what "proportionate" financial-standing diligence looks like for an AI vendor.

### Preconditions
- **Desk-based throughout. No production testing is possible or required**, and none is performed. No live provider system is accessed, probed or contacted; where vendor engagement is needed at all it runs through the firm's own vendor-management channel, initiated by the firm.
- The **AI inventory** (per IRQ-GOV-002), enlarged by TP-TPR-003's embedded-AI findings, and the outsourcing or third-party register — the shared population for the domain, whose completeness is established and challenged once at the start and is a stated dependency and a stated limitation on everything below. The sampling frame is the independently reconciled vendor population, not the register as presented.
- The firm's materiality tiering for AI-dependent business activities, so that "proportionate" has a referent. Where no tiering exists, proportionality cannot be assessed and that is recorded before sampling.
- Read access to: the firm's due diligence policy, specifically whether it names financial standing, funding runway and management integrity as required criteria for AI vendors; completed due diligence files for sampled vendors; executed contracts and any schedules covering data, model, prompt and configuration retrieval and exit; continuity or substitution documentation for the highest-materiality dependencies and any evidence of testing it; refresh records for any financial-standing review; the outsourcing/vendor register showing review status over the last 12 months.
- Personnel: lead reviewer with third-party-risk and outsourcing-regulation competence; legal or procurement support for the contractual limb; the third-party risk manager; the owner of the dependent business activity for the continuation limb, who is the only person able to speak to whether a substitute is dimensioned for the volume.
- Confidentiality: contracts, DPAs and provider due-diligence files are commercially sensitive. Extracts are referenced by clause and stored in the controlled evidence store; the report cites clause references, never reproduced commercial terms.
- **Run with TP-TPR-001 and TP-TPR-002 on the same sampled files where all three are in scope.** This procedure tests a different question against the same population and the same artefacts, and reviewing those files three times is waste.

### Test design
**The distinctness test is the point.** The canvas requires financial standing, funding runway and management integrity to be assessed **as a distinct item alongside — not folded into — safety and security diligence**. A due diligence file in which financial commentary appears as a sentence inside a security assessment does not meet this; a file that addresses only safety, security and model-weakness risk is the canvas's headline fail indicator.

**Four limbs against a materiality-stratified sample.**
- **F1 Financial-standing diligence.** Was financial standing, funding runway and management integrity assessed as a distinct, documented item for critical-tier AI vendors, proportionate to the materiality of the dependent business activity? Record the assessment as it stands, not whether the reviewer agrees with its conclusion — this procedure tests whether the question was asked and answered to policy standard, not whether the vendor is in fact solvent.
- **F2 Contractual retrieval rights.** Are data, model, prompt and configuration retrieval rights evidenced in the executed contract, with return or deletion on exit? Assess these against a **disorderly** exit — insolvency, sudden wind-down — not only the orderly scenario (non-renewal, planned exit) the clause was probably drafted for. The canvas names that conflation as a specific blind spot.
- **F3 Continuation path.** For the highest-materiality dependencies, does a documented continuation path exist, in-house or via a substitute provider, and was the disorderly-exit scenario considered? Assess credibility: is the substitute technically viable, contractually available, and dimensioned for the volume? Cross-reference IRQ-RES-001 where a tested continuation path is claimed.
- **F4 Refresh cadence.** Is financial-standing review defined and evidenced as recurring, with a trigger on change in vendor circumstances — or a one-time check at onboarding? The canvas records a one-time check with no refresh mechanism as a fail indicator in its own right, separate from whether the original check was adequate.

**Population challenge.** Per the standing TPR discipline, the register is both the population and under test. Where a critical-tier AI dependency appears in the architecture but not in the outsourcing register, that omission is reported as a finding regardless of what the sampled files show.

**Legacy limb.** Establish whether financial-standing diligence, where it exists, was introduced under the current framework and applied only to vendors onboarded since — the canvas's own partial condition. Legacy relationships carrying the highest materiality are the ones that matter.

**Scope boundary.** Safety, security and model-weakness diligence is TP-TPR-001's subject, and the general contract-clause walkthrough is TP-TPR-002's. Both are cross-referenced here, not repeated.

### Steps
1. Reconcile the AI vendor population against contracts, expenditure and the outsourcing register, incorporating TP-TPR-003's embedded-AI findings, and challenge completeness. **Record:** the reconciled population, any register gaps, and the known limitations as a stated constraint on everything that follows.
2. Obtain the firm's due diligence policy. **Record:** whether it names financial standing, funding runway and management integrity as required assessment criteria for AI vendors, proportionate to materiality — and whether "proportionate" is defined against a materiality tiering that actually exists.
3. Draw and record the materiality-stratified vendor sample, weighted to critical-tier dependencies, and confirm it is the same sample used for TP-TPR-001 and TP-TPR-002 where those are in scope. **Record:** the sampling frame and rationale.
4. **F1:** For each sampled vendor, inspect the due diligence file. **Record:** whether financial standing, funding runway and management integrity appear as a distinct documented item; where they appear if not; and where the file addresses only safety, security and model-weakness risk.
5. **F4:** Record the date of each financial-standing assessment, whether a refresh cadence is defined and evidenced as followed, whether a trigger on change in vendor circumstances exists, or whether the check was one-time at onboarding.
6. **F2:** For each sampled vendor, inspect the executed contract for data, model, prompt and configuration retrieval rights and any exit-strategy provision, cross-referenced to TP-TPR-002 where that programme has already walked the same contracts. **Record:** clause references or gaps, and separately whether the provisions are drafted for, or would operate in, a disorderly exit as against an orderly one.
7. **F3:** For the highest-materiality dependencies, obtain the continuation or substitution documentation and any test evidence. **Record:** whether a path exists, whether the disorderly-exit scenario was considered, whether it has been tested even at desk-based walkthrough level, and the reviewer's assessment of its credibility on technical viability, contractual availability and volume dimensioning.
8. Cross-check the sampled population against the architecture for critical-tier AI dependencies absent from the register. **Record:** any omission as a finding in its own right.
9. **Legacy limb:** establish whether financial-standing diligence was introduced under the current framework and whether it has been extended retrospectively. **Record:** the coverage gap and the materiality of the vendors sitting in it.
10. Review the outsourcing/vendor register for financial-standing review status and continuity-plan status across all in-scope AI vendors over the last 12 months. **Record:** coverage and refresh evidence.
11. Report exceptions per vendor against each of the four limbs, with the proportion of critical-tier vendors failing each.

### Observations & measures
- **F1 distinctness** (categorical, per vendor): financial standing, funding runway and management integrity assessed as a distinct documented item / addressed only incidentally within safety or security diligence / absent entirely. Canvas pass indicator requires the first for critical-tier vendors; canvas fail indicator is a file addressing only safety, security and model-weakness diligence.
- **F1 coverage** = critical-tier AI vendors with a distinct documented financial-standing assessment ÷ critical-tier AI vendors sampled. Reported as a proportion alongside per-vendor findings; **no FAIAS threshold applies — the canvas sets no percentage, and no FS benchmark exists for what "proportionate" means here.**
- **F2 retrieval rights** (categorical, per vendor): data, model, prompt and configuration retrieval rights contractually evidenced / partially evidenced (naming which artefact classes are covered) / absent. Canvas fail indicator: no contractual retrieval rights for a critical-tier vendor, reliance assuming indefinite vendor availability.
- **F2 disorderly-exit fitness** (binary): the provisions are assessed as operable in a disorderly exit / drafted only against an orderly termination scenario. Recorded as diagnostic context; the canvas names the conflation as a blind spot rather than as a separate fail indicator, and it is reported as such.
- **F3 continuation path** (categorical): documented and tested, at least at desk-based walkthrough level, with the disorderly scenario considered / documented but never tested / none exists. Canvas fail indicator: none exists for a critical-tier vendor. Credibility — technical viability, contractual availability, volume dimensioning — is recorded alongside existence, not merged into it.
- **F4 refresh** (categorical): defined cadence, evidenced as followed / one-time check at onboarding with no refresh mechanism / no financial-standing review at all. Canvas fail indicator: the second.
- **Register omissions** — count of critical-tier AI dependencies identified in the architecture but absent from the outsourcing register.

### Interpretation
- **Pass** — financial standing, funding runway and management integrity are assessed as a distinct, documented item in the due diligence file for critical-tier AI vendors, separate from safety and security diligence; contractual data, model and configuration retrieval rights are evidenced, and a continuation path, in-house or via a substitute provider, is documented for the highest-materiality dependencies; and the refresh cadence for financial-standing review is defined and evidenced, not a one-time check at onboarding.
- **Fail** — due diligence files address only safety, security and model-weakness diligence, with no financial-standing or continuity content; **or** no contractual retrieval rights or continuation path exist for a critical-tier vendor, so reliance assumes indefinite vendor availability; **or** financial-standing review, where it exists, is a one-time check with no refresh mechanism as vendor circumstances change.
- **Partial** — the canvas's own shape: financial-standing diligence has been introduced for vendors onboarded under the current framework but has not been extended retrospectively to legacy relationships. Remediation should prioritise the highest-materiality legacy dependencies first, and the report should name them.

### Common pitfalls & limitations
- **Assessing the vendor's solvency rather than the firm's diligence.** This procedure tests whether the question was asked and answered to policy standard. Forming a credit view on the vendor is neither the reviewer's role nor within the assurance objective.
- **Assuming financial-standing diligence sits elsewhere.** The canvas's own blind-spot finding: AI-specific diligence concentrates on safety, security and model weakness, and financial-standing review is easily assumed to belong to a separate procurement or credit-risk process not tailored to AI-specific continuity risk — so it falls between the two.
- **Accepting financial commentary inside a security assessment.** The canvas requires a distinct item. A sentence about funding inside a security write-up evidences that someone noticed, not that the control operates.
- **Testing exit clauses against an orderly scenario only.** Insolvency and sudden wind-down behave very differently from planned non-renewal, and the clause was probably drafted for the latter. The canvas names this specifically.
- **Trusting vendor-supplied funding material.** Unverified investor-relations content is L1; the evidence is the firm's own completed assessment.
- **Sampling only vendors onboarded recently.** Financial-standing diligence is typically introduced with a framework refresh and applied prospectively; the legacy relationships it never touched are usually the largest.
- **Treating a strong safety and security posture as reassurance.** The two are independent. A vendor can be excellent on both and commercially fragile, and a reviewer satisfied on one may not think to check the other — the canvas's blind-spot finding.
- **Re-testing TP-TPR-001/002 content.** Safety and security diligence and the general contract-clause walkthrough are covered by those programmes and are cross-referenced, not repeated.
- **Aggregating an incomplete register.** This procedure inherits every gap in the AI inventory, and undiscovered embedded AI tends to sit with exactly the kind of early-stage vendor this procedure exists to find. State the limitation prominently.
- **★1 honesty.** The mechanism is well understood in general vendor risk management, but the AI-vendor-specific evidence base is thin: **no verified incident of an AI vendor failure causing an FS firm continuity impact is cited in the canvas**, and no benchmark exists for what proportionate diligence looks like here. The one case the canvas cites — Mobley v. Workday, Inc. (N.D. Cal., 3:23-cv-00770-RFL, order of 12 July 2024) — is cited only for the narrower principle that an AI vendor's own conduct and standing can become the firm's direct exposure; it concerns discrimination liability, not vendor insolvency or business continuity, and is not relied on as evidence of the continuity-on-failure mechanism. Report as professional assessment, not a certified compliance determination.

### Time/effort estimate
**3.0 reviewer-days** — 0.5 register reconciliation, population challenge and policy/materiality-tiering review; 1.0 F1 due-diligence file inspection across the sample (the dominant cost, scaling with vendor count); 0.5 F2 contract inspection including the disorderly-exit assessment; 0.5 F3 continuation-path review and credibility assessment for the highest-materiality dependencies; 0.5 F4 refresh, legacy and register-omission limbs and write-up. **Runs efficiently on the same materiality-stratified sample and the same due-diligence and contract files as TP-TPR-001 and TP-TPR-002 (combined saving of approximately 1.0 day across the three programmes).** Excludes report drafting and firm-side effort.
