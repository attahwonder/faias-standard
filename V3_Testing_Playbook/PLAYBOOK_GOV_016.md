# TP-GOV-016 — AI-Originated Content in the Risk-Data Aggregation Chain

## 1. Procedure Identity
**Procedure ID:** TP-GOV-016
**IRQ:** IRQ-GOV-016 — AI Output Entering Risk-Data Aggregation and Regulatory Reporting Without BCBS 239 Discipline
**Assurance objective:** AO-GOV-016 — Where AI-generated or AI-assisted output feeds the firm's risk-data aggregation estate, capital or provisioning calculations, or supervisory/regulatory returns, that output shall be subject to the same accuracy, completeness, timeliness, adaptability and traceability discipline as other risk data, and its AI origin shall be flagged and reconstructable within the aggregation record.
**★ Confidence (carried from canvas, unchanged):** ★2 — the standard being extended, BCBS 239, is mature and regulator-mandated, but **no established methodology or precedent yet exists for how its principles apply specifically to AI-generated contributions**, so the extension itself rests on reasoned analogy rather than settled practice.
**Method classification:** Emerging Practice — a documentation and traceability review against an existing regulatory standard, not a new empirical test; the canvas records that no published testing methodology exists specific to AI content in risk-data aggregation and regulatory reporting. The **standard it is run against** is the Basel Committee's *Principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting* (BCBS 239, January 2013): 14 principles across governance and infrastructure, risk data aggregation capabilities, risk reporting practices and supervisory review, initially binding on global systemically important banks from 1 January 2016. **The extension of those principles to AI-originated content is reasoned analogy, not settled practice, and is stated as such rather than presented as an existing requirement of the framework.** The flagging mechanism, the accuracy-equivalence control and the traceability extension are each marked Emerging Practice in the canvas's control list.

## 2. Preconditions
**Environment:** Documentation, configuration and sample tracing. **No production system access is required**, and none is taken. Tracing runs against retained aggregation records and documentation; no return or calculation is altered and no regulator is contacted.
**Direction bound, confirmed at scoping.** Existing input-side data controls elsewhere in the standard govern **data flowing into the model**. This procedure tests **model output flowing into the firm's own risk-reporting estate** — a different direction entirely, and the canvas records the conflation of the two as a specific blind spot. An existing clean result on input-side data controls is not evidence here.
**Access required:** The risk-data aggregation estate's documentation, specifically any flagging or tagging mechanism for AI-originated content and where AI-generated or AI-assisted content enters; the accuracy, completeness, timeliness and adaptability controls applied to aggregation content, and the equivalent controls applied to system-sourced data as the comparison baseline; configuration or process access sufficient to confirm the flag is actually applied at the point of entry; the aggregation record itself, at a level permitting a trace from a regulatory return or capital/provisioning calculation back through it; the AI systems and versions producing narrative, summarisation or reconciliation content that feeds the estate; human sign-off records where AI-assisted content is approved before entry.
**Artefacts required:** Documentation of the flagging/tagging mechanism and of the controls applied to AI-originated content; the inventory of AI systems contributing to the aggregation estate, by content type — narrative risk commentary, exposure summarisation, reconciliation output; a sample of AI-originated content spanning all three types, with the aggregation record for each; at least one regulatory return or capital/provisioning calculation from the review period, with its supporting aggregation record; ownership records for extending aggregation discipline to AI-originated content; any internal audit or independent review of aggregation-framework coverage for AI content.
**Personnel required:** Reviewer (lead); the risk-data aggregation or RDARR framework owner; the owner of the regulatory return or calculation traced; the owner of each AI system contributing sampled content; internal audit contact where a prior review exists; the named owner for extending aggregation discipline to AI-originated content, **if one exists** — whether that ownership exists distinctly, or defaults to the general framework owner with no AI-specific consideration, is one of the findings.
**Data handling:** Aggregation records and regulatory returns contain confidential prudential data. Handle under engagement confidentiality; the report cites the trace path, the flagging status and the control evidence, not the underlying exposure figures.

## 3. Test Design
**A flagging-mechanism review and a sample trace through the aggregation record, run in both directions.** The canvas defines the test as a documentation and traceability review; there is no empirical or system-behaviour limb, and the procedure introduces none.

**Forward sample.** The reviewer selects a sample of **AI-originated content contributing to the risk-data aggregation estate**, spanning the three content types the canvas names — a narrative risk commentary, an exposure summarisation, and a reconciliation output. The canvas's pass indicator requires the flagging mechanism to cover all three, so the sample spans all three. Sampling only one type is the canvas's own partial condition: narrative commentary flagged and traceable while reconciliation output from the same AI system is indistinguishable from system-sourced data.

**Three questions per sampled item.**
1. **Is it flagged** as AI-originated at the point it enters the aggregation chain, distinguishing it from system-sourced data?
2. **Were accuracy, completeness, timeliness and adaptability controls equivalent to those applied to system-sourced data actually applied to it** — evidenced, dimension by dimension, not assumed because the content is a summary or reconciliation rather than a raw figure?
3. **Is the chain reconstructable** back to the AI system, its version, and any human sign-off that produced or approved the content?

**Implementation check, distinct from the documentation check.** Configuration or process review confirms the flagging mechanism is **actually implemented and applied at the point of entry**, not documented only. A documented mechanism that is not applied produces the same aggregation record as no mechanism at all.

**Reverse trace.** From at least one regulatory return or capital/provisioning calculation in the review period, trace back through the aggregation record and establish whether AI-originated inputs are identifiable and whether their accuracy was controlled. This runs in the opposite direction from the forward sample and is what catches AI-originated content the forward sample's inventory did not know about — the forward sample is drawn from an inventory the firm supplied, and the reverse trace is not.

**Ownership limb.** Establish whether named ownership exists for extending aggregation discipline to AI-originated content, distinct from ownership of the aggregation framework generally. The canvas records ownership that "defaults to the general aggregation-framework owner with no AI-specific consideration" as a fail indicator in its own right.

**Historical limb.** Applying "existence is not operation": establish whether at least one historical instance evidences AI-originated content correctly flagged, controlled and traced through to a regulatory return or calculation. The canvas records the absence of any such instance as a fail indicator, separate from the mechanism's documented existence.

## 4. Steps
1. Confirm the direction bound with the framework owner and record it: this tests output flowing into risk reporting, not data flowing into models.
2. Build the inventory of AI systems producing narrative, summarisation or reconciliation content that feeds the aggregation estate, and map where that content enters. **Record:** the entry-point inventory by content type, whether the firm's own documentation already identifies each, and any content type with no identified producing system — which is itself a traceability finding.
3. Obtain the flagging/tagging mechanism documentation and the documented controls applied to AI-originated content. **Record:** what is claimed, per content type.
4. Confirm by configuration or process review that the flagging mechanism is actually implemented and applied at the point of entry. **Record:** implemented and applied / documented but not applied / absent.
5. Draw and record the forward sample across all three content types. **Record:** the sampling basis and content-type coverage.
6. For each sampled item, establish whether it is flagged as AI-originated in the aggregation record. **Record:** flagged / indistinguishable from system-sourced data, per item.
7. For each sampled item, establish whether accuracy, completeness, timeliness and adaptability controls equivalent to those applied to system-sourced data were applied — and obtain the evidence, not the assertion. **Record:** per principle, the control evidence — equivalent and evidenced / weaker / absent / assumed.
8. For each sampled item, trace back to the producing AI system, its version, and any human sign-off. **Record:** the reconstructable chain, or the point at which it breaks.
9. Run the reverse trace: from a regulatory return or capital/provisioning calculation in the review period, trace back through the aggregation record and establish whether AI-originated inputs are identifiable and their accuracy was controlled. **Record:** the trace, and any AI-originated content found that the step 2 inventory did not include.
10. Establish named ownership for extending aggregation discipline to AI-originated content. **Record:** the named owner, or that it defaults to the general framework owner with no AI-specific consideration.
11. Search for at least one historical instance where AI-originated content was correctly flagged, controlled and traced through to a regulatory return or calculation. **Record:** the instance, or its absence.
12. Where any sampled item or reverse-trace finding is unflagged or uncontrolled, establish whether any return or calculation already submitted incorporates it. **Record:** the position — a submitted return resting on unflagged content is a materially different finding from a gap caught before submission.

## 6. Interpretation (per CANVASES_GOV.md fields 12–14)
**Pass** where sampled AI-originated content in the aggregation chain is flagged as such and traceable back to the producing system, version and sign-off; accuracy, completeness, timeliness and adaptability controls equivalent to those applied to system-sourced data are evidenced rather than assumed; at least one historical instance evidences AI-originated content correctly flagged, controlled and traced through to a regulatory return or capital/provisioning calculation; named ownership exists for extending aggregation discipline to AI-originated content; and the flagging mechanism covers narrative, summarisation and reconciliation content rather than only one type.

**Fail** where: sampled AI-originated content in the aggregation chain has no flag distinguishing it from system-sourced data; **or** accuracy or traceability controls applied to it are assumed rather than evidenced; **or** no mechanism exists to trace AI-originated content back through the aggregation record to the producing system, version or sign-off; **or** no historical instance can be produced showing the flagging or control mechanism actually operating; **or** ownership for extending aggregation discipline to AI-originated content is undefined or defaults to the general framework owner with no AI-specific consideration.

**Partial** where — the canvas's own shape — narrative risk commentary is flagged and traceable, but reconciliation output produced by the same AI system is not distinguished from system-sourced data in the aggregation record. The assurance objective is not met for reconciliation content; remediation should extend the flagging mechanism to cover all AI-originated content types. Report by content type, and state whether any already-submitted return incorporates unflagged content — that determines whether remediation is a process fix or requires a restatement conversation.

## 7. Evidence Outputs
- Statement that the firm complies with its risk-data aggregation framework — **L1** (asserts general compliance; does not evidence AI-content coverage specifically)
- Documentation of the flagging/tagging mechanism for AI-originated content, and of the accuracy, completeness, timeliness and adaptability controls applied to it — **L2**
- Inventory of AI systems contributing to the aggregation estate, by content type, with entry points — **L2**
- Configuration or process review confirming the flagging mechanism is actually implemented and applied at the point of entry — **L3**

- Reverse trace from a regulatory return or capital/provisioning calculation back through the aggregation record, confirming AI-originated inputs are identifiable and their accuracy was controlled, and recording any content the forward inventory omitted — **L3**
- Ownership record for extending aggregation discipline to AI-originated content — **L2/L3**
- Internal audit or independent review of aggregation-framework coverage specifically for AI-originated content — **L4**
- Evidence from an actual historical instance showing AI-originated content correctly flagged, controlled and traced through to a regulatory return or calculation — **L5**

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Limitations
- **Treating input-side data controls as coverage.** They govern data flowing into the model. This is model output flowing into the firm's own risk-reporting estate — a different direction, and the canvas names the conflation specifically. Step 1 exists to close it off before fieldwork.
- **"The model summarised, it didn't decide."** Intuitive and incomplete. Inaccurate summarisation or reconciliation content can corrupt a capital or provisioning figure exactly as a data error would, and the aggregation framework's discipline exists for that reason regardless of how the content was produced.
- **Framework-compliance assertions.** Compliance for system-sourced data says nothing about AI-originated content; the framework predates that content type entirely.
- **Accepting a documented flagging mechanism.** A mechanism not applied at the point of entry produces an aggregation record indistinguishable from one with no mechanism. Step 4 is the difference between a documented control and an operating one.
- **Sampling narrative content only.** Narrative commentary is the AI contribution most obvious to a reviewer. Reconciliation output looks least like "data" produced by a model and is the canvas's own partial condition.
- **Running the forward sample only.** The forward sample is drawn from an inventory the firm supplied. The reverse trace is what finds AI-originated content the firm did not know was in its aggregation chain, and that discovery is frequently the substantive finding.
- **Accepting an assertion of control equivalence.** The canvas requires the controls to be evidenced, dimension by dimension, not assumed because the content is a summary rather than a raw figure.
- **Missing the submitted-return question.** A gap caught before submission is a process fix. A submitted return resting on unflagged, uncontrolled AI-originated content raises a restatement question that a later fix to the flagging mechanism does not undo. Step 12 separates the two.
- **Regulatory-basis limitation carried forward, and it is central to this procedure.** The framework's principles were written for system-sourced risk data. **No provision within the framework, and no subsequent regulatory instrument identified in the canvas's verified source set, explicitly extends them to AI-generated or AI-assisted content.** The control objective rests on reasoned analogy — that content contributing to the same aggregation estate should meet the same discipline regardless of how it was produced. Report it that way; do not present the finding as non-compliance with an existing AI-specific requirement, because there is none.
- **★2 caveat.** A documentation and traceability review extending a mature, regulator-mandated framework to AI content **by reasoned analogy, not settled practice**. Report as professional assessment of the sample reviewed, and carry the reasoned-analogy qualifier into the conclusion.

## 9. Time / Effort Estimate
**4 reviewer-days**: 0.5 day direction-bound confirmation and AI-contribution entry-point inventory; 0.5 day flagging-mechanism documentation review and implementation check; 1 day forward sample across the three content types, with control evidence gathered dimension by dimension; 1 day reverse trace from a regulatory return through the aggregation record (the analytical core, and the limb most worth protecting if time is short); 0.5 day ownership, historical-instance and submitted-return limbs; 0.5 day analysis and working papers.
