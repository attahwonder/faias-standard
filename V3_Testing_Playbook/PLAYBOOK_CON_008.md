# TP-CON-008 — AI-Use Disclosure Comprehension Walkthrough

## 1. Procedure Identity
**Procedure ID:** TP-CON-008
**IRQ:** IRQ-CON-008 — Absent or Inadequate Disclosure of Material AI Influence on Customer Decisions
**Assurance objective:** AO-CON-008 — Where AI materially influences a decision or interaction affecting a customer, the firm shall provide disclosure that is present at the relevant point and comprehensible to the actual customer population, tested rather than merely asserted.
**★ Confidence (carried from canvas, unchanged):** ★2 — Consumer Duty comprehension-testing methodology is established generally for customer communications (★3 for that general component); testing specifically for AI-influence comprehension is a newer, bespoke application with no agreed FS benchmark — the blended rating reflects the more novel component.
**Method classification:** Adapted Industry Practice (Consumer Duty communications comprehension testing) applied to a novel disclosure object.

## 2. Preconditions
**Environment:** Non-production reproduction of the customer journey at the point of the material interaction, presenting the disclosure exactly as a real customer would encounter it — same placement, same typography, same surrounding content, same channel. A disclosure tested as an isolated paragraph tests a different artefact from the one customers actually see.
**Access required:** Ability to reproduce each in-scope customer journey to the disclosure point; read access to the disclosure content across all touchpoints (T&Cs, privacy notice, point-of-interaction); read access to the contest/human-re-decision route design and its actual usage volumes.
**Artefacts required:** All disclosure text as currently delivered; journey screenshots or recordings at the disclosure point; the contest route design and signposting; complaint records relating to AI awareness; the firm's own consumer-testing methodology for other communications (as the comparison standard).
**Personnel required:** A participant panel (see design); a facilitator experienced in comprehension testing; the customer communications owner; reviewer.
**Data handling:** Participant recruitment and testing engage personal data and require their own lawful basis, participant information and consent. Where recruiting real customers is infeasible, use a matched panel and record the matching basis and its limitations. No participant makes a real financial decision and no live decisioning is affected; all scenarios are clearly framed as test scenarios at debrief.

## 3. Test Design
Structured comprehension testing at the point of a material interaction. Three in-scope interaction types at minimum (reviewer-set design minimum, disclosed at scoping), chosen to span the range:

- An **adverse decision** (e.g. a credit decline) — the highest-stakes case for contest-route awareness.
- A **price or offer presentation** — where influence is material but non-adverse.
- A **conversational interaction** (e.g. a chatbot-drafted response) — where AI involvement is least visible.

Each participant encounters the journey and then answers structured questions testing two things the canvas names specifically: (a) did AI materially influence this outcome, and (b) does a contest/human-re-decision route exist and how would you access it. Comprehension is scored against a rubric fixed before testing.

Panel: minimum 20 participants per interaction type (reviewer-set design minimum, disclosed at scoping), recruited to reflect the actual customer population on the dimensions that plausibly affect comprehension — age, digital confidence, financial literacy, first language. A panel of digitally confident participants will report high comprehension and tell the firm nothing about the customers most at risk.

A separate limb, easily missed, addresses **human-sign-off interactions**: where a human formally makes the decision but the AI substantially shaped their judgement, is disclosure given at all? The canvas treats treating these as requiring no disclosure as a fail indicator.

## 4. Steps
1. Enumerate every material AI-influenced decision/interaction type. **Record:** the list, cross-referenced to the AI inventory, and which are in scope for testing.
2. Obtain all disclosure text across every touchpoint. **Record:** where each disclosure appears (T&Cs, privacy notice, point-of-interaction) and its wording. Record specifically whether any point-of-interaction disclosure exists at all, distinct from generic T&Cs.
3. Reproduce the journey to the disclosure point for each in-scope interaction type. **Record:** screenshots/recordings evidencing that the reproduction matches the live journey.
4. Fix the comprehension rubric and the pass threshold before testing. **Record:** the questions, the scoring rubric, the threshold, and the reasoning — cross-referenced to the firm's own methodology for other Consumer Duty communications testing where one exists.
5. Recruit the panel to the specified composition. **Record:** achieved composition against target on each dimension; any dimension where recruitment fell short.
6. Run the sessions. For each participant and interaction type, present the journey and administer the structured questions. **Record:** verbatim responses; rubric scores for question (a) AI influence and question (b) contest route.
7. Compute comprehension rates per interaction type and per participant dimension. **Record:** rates and counts; specifically the rates for lower-digital-confidence and lower-financial-literacy participants, which typically diverge sharply from the aggregate.
8. **Human-sign-off limb.** Identify interactions where a human formally decides but AI substantially shapes the judgement. **Record:** whether any disclosure is given; the firm's stated rationale; and the reviewer's assessment of the degree of AI influence in those interactions.
9. Test contest-route signposting. **Record:** whether the contest route is signposted alongside the disclosure or delivered separately/omitted; and from the sessions, whether participants could describe how to access it.
10. Review contest route usage volumes and complaint records. **Record:** actual contest volumes relative to adverse-decision volumes; any complaints indicating customers were unaware AI was involved in a decision they were trying to contest — the canvas's most direct real-world fail indicator.
11. Debrief all participants that the scenarios were tests with no real effect. **Record:** debrief completion.

## 5. Observations & Measures
| Measure | Threshold | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Comprehension of material AI influence | The firm's own defined, documented threshold. Where none exists, the reviewer sets and records one before testing and reports it as reviewer-set | Per canvas pass indicator ("a defined, documented threshold of customer understanding"); no external FS benchmark for AI-influence comprehension |
| Comprehension of contest route availability | As above | Per canvas |
| Comprehension gap across participant dimensions | Reviewer judgement — no established threshold. Report descriptively; the gap for low-digital-confidence participants is the finding of practical interest | Bespoke |
| Point-of-interaction disclosure present | Required for all material AI-influenced interaction types, distinct from generic T&Cs | Categorical per canvas fail indicator |
| Human-sign-off interactions assessed for AI influence | Assessment performed and material influence disclosed | Categorical per canvas fail indicator |
| Contest-route signposting paired with disclosure | Present | Categorical per canvas pass indicator |

## 6. Interpretation (per CANVASES_CON.md fields 12–14)
**Pass** where: point-of-interaction disclosure exists for all material AI-influenced decision/interaction types, distinct from generic T&Cs; comprehension testing demonstrates a defined documented threshold of customer understanding of AI's material role and the availability of a contest route; human-sign-off interactions have been assessed for degree of AI influence with material influence disclosed rather than assumed covered by "a human decided"; and contest-route signposting is paired with the disclosure.

**Fail** where: disclosure exists only in generic T&Cs or privacy-notice language with no point-of-interaction delivery for material decisions; no comprehension testing has been performed and adequacy is asserted on legal-sufficiency review alone; human-sign-off interactions are treated as requiring no AI disclosure regardless of how substantially the AI shaped the judgement; or customers report via complaints or queries being unaware AI was materially involved in a decision they are trying to contest.

**Partial** where point-of-interaction disclosure exists and tests adequately for the adverse-decision journey but is absent for conversational interactions, or where comprehension of AI influence passes while comprehension of the contest route fails — a very common split.

## 7. Evidence Outputs
- Disclosure text inventory across touchpoints and journey reproduction evidence — **L2**
- Comprehension rubric and threshold, recorded pre-testing — **L2**
- Session transcripts and per-participant rubric scores — **L3**
- Comprehension rates by interaction type and participant dimension — **L3**
- Reviewer's independent disclosure adequacy assessment and human-sign-off influence assessment — **L4**
- Contest route usage volumes and complaint records evidencing real customer awareness — **L5**

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Limitations
- **Testing the disclosure out of context.** A paragraph shown in isolation is comprehended far better than the same paragraph in a live journey. Reproduce the journey.
- **A convenience panel.** Colleagues, or a panel skewed to digitally confident participants, will produce a passing result that is worthless. Step 5's composition record is the safeguard.
- **Setting the rubric after seeing responses.** Same failure mode as post-hoc metric selection; fix it at step 4.
- **Omitting the human-sign-off limb.** This is the limb firms most often have no answer to, and the one the canvas explicitly names.
- **Small panels reported with false precision.** Twenty participants supports a directional finding, not a percentage to one decimal place. Report ranges and counts.
- **Participant welfare.** Adverse-decision scenarios can distress participants who have experienced real declines. Frame as test scenarios, brief and debrief properly, and record that this was done.
- **★2 caveat.** Comprehension testing is established; AI-influence comprehension testing is not. Report as professional assessment.

## 9. Time / Effort Estimate
**4 reviewer-days** of reviewer time: 0.5 day disclosure inventory, 0.5 day rubric design, 1.5 days session facilitation and observation across three interaction types, 1 day analysis, 0.5 day working papers. Note that panel recruitment is typically 2–3 weeks of elapsed time and carries a separate cost line outside reviewer-days.
